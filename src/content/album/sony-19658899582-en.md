## Bach Is a Blue Without a Name

If you laid music history out as a row of color swatches, Bach would sit in the exact middle: not a vivid red, not a deep black, but a blue that can take on any emotion you stain it with.

<!-- fiction: metaphor comparing Bach's place in music history to a neutral color -->

That is exactly what Norwegian violinist Eldbjørg Hemsing and the Norwegian String Quintet do on *Colors of Bach* — twenty-three dye experiments on that blue.

<!-- fact: The album is a Bach arrangement collection by Hemsing and the Norwegian String Quintet, 23 tracks, released Jan 16, 2026 on Sony Classical -->

## Not Copying — Recoloring

The word "Colors" in the title is not decorative. The album brought in three arrangers — Tim Allhoff, Jan-Peter Klöpfel, and Jarkko Riihimäki — to "recolor" roughly twenty of Bach's best-known melodies into contemporary textures.

<!-- fact: The three arrangers are Tim Allhoff, Jan-Peter Klöpfel, and Jarkko Riihimäki -->

The Goldberg Variations' Aria becomes a jazz-tinted piano miniature; the First Brandenburg Concerto is turned into a frolic for string quintet; that gentlest of chorales from Cantata BWV 147 — *Jesu, Joy of Man's Desiring* — is tuned down to near chamber-music transparency.

<!-- fact: Arrangements draw on the Goldberg Variations, Brandenburg Concerto No. 1, and Cantata BWV 147, among others -->

Interestingly, there are no cello suites here — the soloist is a violin. All twenty-three tracks grow from Bach melodies, closing with a faithful account of the complete *Violin Concerto No. 2 in E major* (BWV 1042), the album's un-altered anchor point.

<!-- fact: All 23 tracks are Bach arrangements; the album ends with the complete three-movement Violin Concerto BWV 1042, played as written -->

<!-- fiction: framing the album's identity and the absence of cello suites -->

## A 1707 Stradivarius

Hemsing was born in Valdres, Norway, studied in Vienna under Boris Kuschnir, and plays the 1707 Stradivarius "Rivaz, Baron Gutmann." Her tone is sumptuous and warm; next to the quintet's billowing texture, Bach's lines feel breathed rather than engraved.

<!-- fact: Hemsing born 1990 in Valdres; studied with Boris Kuschnir; plays the 1707 Stradivarius 'Rivaz, Baron Gutmann' -->

She has a history of firsts: among the premiere performers of Tan Dun's Violin Concerto, and a recording artist of *Fire Ritual*. *Colors of Bach* is her dive into Bach's ocean — not a conquest, but a dyeing.

<!-- fact: Hemsing has long partnered with Tan Dun, premiering and recording his violin works The Love and Fire Ritual -->

## Hemsing's Two Taipei Nights

August 2024, Taipei's Zhongshan Hall. Two concerts in the Taipei Symphony Orchestra's "Summer Music Stroll" series: the first night Sibelius's Violin Concerto; the second, the Taiwan premiere of Tan Dun's *Fire Ritual*.

<!-- fact: Aug 17–18, 2024 Hemsing performed with the TSO at Zhongshan Hall; Aug 18 was the Taiwan premiere of Tan Dun's Fire Ritual -->

Taiwanese audiences' acquaintance with that Stradivarius was sealed in those two days.

## Further Listening

For another road of "Bach in modern translation," Yo-Yo Ma's 2018 remake of the cello suites is a completely different route — he stripped away bowings and breathing marks and re-read the score through decades of experience. Jan Lisiecki's *Preludes*, meanwhile, sets Bach's preludes beside Chopin and Rachmaninoff, letting you hear one seed growing into different branches across three centuries.

<!-- fact: Yo-Yo Ma's 2018 re-recording removed some bowing articulations; Lisiecki's Preludes album pairs Bach with Chopin and Rachmaninoff preludes -->
