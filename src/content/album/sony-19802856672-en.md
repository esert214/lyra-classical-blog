## A Genius's Late Years

The Liszt we know is the virtuoso piano master—those dazzling transcendental etudes and flamboyant transcriptions.

<!-- fact: Liszt is known as a virtuoso piano master -->

But in his late years, Liszt took another path. He devoutly converted to Catholicism, and his music turned to deep introspection.

<!-- fact: Late Liszt converted to Catholicism, and his music turned inward -->

And Via Crucis is the finest representative of this period.

<!-- fact: Via Crucis is a late, introspective religious masterpiece by Liszt -->

## The Way of Sorrows

"Via Crucis" means "Way of the Cross"—the fourteen stations of Jesus carrying the cross to Golgotha.

<!-- fact: Via Crucis refers to the fourteen stations of the cross -->

Liszt's Via Crucis interweaves Gregorian chant with minimalist piano texture, depicting this path. It is not a showy work—it is plain, introspective, yet deeply spiritual.

<!-- fact: Via Crucis interweaves Gregorian chant with minimalist texture, plain and introspective -->

Andsnes lets this work flow from his fingers, restrained yet full of feeling. Those quiet, almost prayerful notes linger long in your ear.

<!-- fiction: commentary on Andsnes's playing -->
