## Swiss Watchmaking Precision and Poetic Madness

Igor Stravinsky once famously dubbed Maurice Ravel "the most exquisite Swiss clockmaker." His piano scores resemble intricate horological movements studded with rubies, where a fraction of a millisecond's error brings ruin.

Italian virtuoso Beatrice Rana’s complete Ravel solo piano works for Warner Classics stands as a crowning achievement of contemporary pianism. At thirty-one, Rana commands an astonishing dynamic palette, transforming Ravel's notoriously punishing technical demands into fluid, shimmering watercolors.

## Conquering Gaspard de la nuit

Every Ravel interpreter must confront the sheer vertical precipice of *Gaspard de la nuit*. In *Ondine*, Rana’s right-hand ostinato ripples like dawn light across the Seine, feather-light yet impeccably articulated. In *Le Gibet*, the inexorable ticking B-flat bell motif evokes chilling, desolate silence under a grim scaffold.

Then comes *Scarbo*, universally feared as one of the most perilous scores in the repertoire. Rana unleashes a tour-de-force of demonic precision. The phantom goblin’s leaps, scurrying runs, and terrifying fortissimo explosions strike with surgical accuracy wrapped in Gothic terror.

## Twilight Reflections at Wally Records

On Halloween eve, with only an amber desk lamp illuminating the counter, Rana’s Ravel feels uncanny. As the stylus glides across vinyl, Ravel's poisonous, intoxicating harmonies fill the dusk.

This is no cozy hearth fire; it is an ice palace of supreme architectural order. In Rana's hands, perfection itself becomes an intoxicating poetic experience.
