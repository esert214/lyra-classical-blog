## A Wormhole of Light Across French Keyboard History

This is no ordinary recital program; it is an act of alchemical time travel.

Icelandic pianist Víkingur Ólafsson’s celebrated Deutsche Grammophon release *Debussy · Rameau* juxtaposes the eighteenth-century Baroque pioneer Jean-Philippe Rameau with Impressionist innovator Claude Debussy. Gazing through an Icelandic prism of crystalline clarity, Ólafsson shatters conventional musicological categories, demonstrating that these two Parisian minds were drinking from the exact same stream of water, light, and wit.

<!-- fact: Recorded at Harpa Concert Hall in Reykjavík and released by Deutsche Grammophon (483 7701), the album alternates harpsichord pieces by Rameau with piano works by Debussy -->

## Hen Clucks and Moonlight Over Forgotten Pagodas

In Rameau’s *La Poule* (The Hen), Ólafsson translates percussive harpsichord pecks into glittering modern Steinway staccatos—witty yet aristocratic. Without pause, he transitions into Debussy’s *Et la lune descend sur le temple qui fut*, where the tone instantly liquefies into nocturnal stillness.

Ólafsson’s pedal technique is a marvel of restraint. Refusing to hide behind blurred washes of resonance, he shapes color directly from fingertip articulation, allowing polyphonic voices to bloom with architectural purity. Rameau’s *Les Sauvages* and Debussy’s *Des pas sur la neige* sound like two sides of the very same poetic coin.

## Quiet Hours at Wally Records

When late afternoon sun angles across our wooden floorboards, this is the disc I choose. Whenever its opening notes float into the room, quiet descends.
