## A Ship That Never Docks

He cursed himself, so he roams the seas forever. Every seventh year he gets one chance to come ashore, to find a woman who will truly love him.

<!-- fact: The protagonist of The Flying Dutchman is cursed to roam the seas, able to come ashore only once every seven years to find true love -->

This is Wagner's The Flying Dutchman (Der fliegende Holländer), an opera of redemption and sacrifice.

<!-- fact: The Flying Dutchman premiered in Dresden in 1843; its themes are redemption and sacrifice -->

And this 2025 Decca recording brings together some of the brightest names in opera today—Lise Davidsen and Gerald Finley.

<!-- fact: Decca 4870952, released 2025, recorded live in Oslo on August 25, 2024 -->

## A Valkyrie's Voice

Lise Davidsen plays Senta—the girl obsessed with the Dutchman's legend, willing to sacrifice herself for him.

<!-- fact: Lise Davidsen plays Senta -->

Davidsen possesses one of the rarest instruments in opera today: a huge, lustrous dramatic soprano. Here she does not simply "bombard" with full force, but handles Senta's obsession, innocence, and final sacrifice with clear shading.

<!-- fiction: commentary on Davidsen's singing -->

That famous "Senta's Ballad" is, in her hands, both a retelling of the legend and a confession of a girl's inner longing.

<!-- fact: 'Senta's Ballad' is Senta's famous aria at the end of Act I -->

## The Dutchman's Curse

Gerald Finley's Dutchman is the emotional core of the opera.

<!-- fact: Gerald Finley plays the Dutchman -->

Finley's voice is low and resonant; that cursed, weary yet redemption-seeking complexity is rendered with great conviction. His Dutchman is not a simple "tragic hero," but a soul tormented by fate.

<!-- fiction: commentary on Finley's singing -->

## Live in Oslo

This recording was made live at the Norwegian National Opera in Oslo in August 2024, conducted by Edward Gardner, who took over the company in 2024.

<!-- fact: This recording was made live in Oslo on August 25, 2024; Gardner has been music director since 2024 -->

The benefit of a live recording is its theatrical immediacy and energy. The Norwegian National Opera's orchestra and chorus, under Gardner, render Wagner's stormy overture and sailors' chorus with sweeping power.

<!-- opinion: commentary on the live recording's quality -->

This recording won the 2025 Gramophone Best Opera Recording award.

<!-- fact: This recording won the 2025 Gramophone Best Opera Recording award -->

## Why Listen

For many, Wagner's operas are a "high barrier." But The Flying Dutchman is one of his most accessible works—it is shorter, more direct in melody, and its themes are relatively easy to grasp.

<!-- opinion: commentary on accessibility -->

And the pairing of Davidsen and Finley, under Gardner's baton, makes this recording one of the best Dutchmen of recent years.

<!-- opinion: subjective recommendation -->

## Further Listening

If you love this kind of opera of the sea and redemption, Verdi's Simon Boccanegra is another choice. And for Davidsen's dramatic soprano world, Fatma Said's Lieder offers art songs.

<!-- fact: Simon Boccanegra (1857) is on Opera Rara; Fatma Said's Lieder is on Warner Classics -->
