## First, Let's Clear Up Three Errors and One Truth

Chinese-language write-ups of this album are a mess, so let me sweep the mines first.

<!-- fiction: opening framing about misinformation -->

Some call him "Liu Yutong" — wrong. His name is Bruce Xiaoyu Liu, a Canadian pianist of Chinese descent born in Paris in 1997.

<!-- fact: his correct Chinese name is Liu Xiaoyu (Bruce Xiaoyu Liu), a Canadian pianist born in Paris in 1997 -->

Some call him the "2025 Tchaikovsky Competition gold medalist" — wrong. He won the gold medal at the 18th International Chopin Competition in 2021, at just 24, playing Chopin in the finals.

<!-- fact: Liu won gold at the 18th Chopin Competition in 2021 (aged 24), not the Tchaikovsky Competition -->

And some say this album is "Rimsky-Korsakov's Scheherazade plus Ravel" — the most absurd error of all. Scheherazade is an orchestral suite; this is a solo piano album. The two cannot fit inside the same piano.

<!-- fact: Lunaris is a solo piano album and contains no orchestral Scheherazade; much Chinese online coverage contains these errors -->

The truth: this is a concept album about "night and moonlight." Deutsche Grammophon released it on August 7, 2026, recorded at Berlin's Teldex Studio, and Presto picked it as an album of the week.

<!-- fact: Lunaris is a 'night and moonlight' concept album, DG 486 8761, released August 7, 2026, recorded at Teldex Studio, Berlin -->

## Two Textures of Moonlight

The album opens with Chopin's Nocturne Op. 27 No. 2. Liu knows this piece so well he dares to hold each phrase-ending half a beat longer — like a moonlit stroller who suddenly stops to look at the lake.

<!-- fiction: literary description of his playing style -->

Then comes Beethoven's "Moonlight" Sonata. Moonlight appears twice on one album: Chopin's, tender and inward; Beethoven's, meditative and profound. But Liu's truly brilliant programming move is placing the "Waldstein" later — that is dawn, the moment the moon sets and the sun rises. The whole album becomes a single night: dusk, deep night, daybreak.

<!-- fact: the album programs Chopin's Nocturne Op. 27/2 alongside Beethoven's 'Moonlight' and 'Waldstein' Sonatas, tracing an arc from night to dawn -->

## The Moons You Didn't Expect

If it were only Chopin plus Beethoven, this would be just another moonlight compilation. Its real ambition hides in the second half.

<!-- fiction: pivot to the album's distinctiveness -->

Debussy's Rêverie dissolves like mist; Bach's prelude (in Siloti's arrangement) stands solemn as a moonlit chapel; Ligeti's "Fanfares" suddenly switches the rhythm on, like the moon shattered by clouds and reassembled.

<!-- fact: the album also includes Debussy's Rêverie, Bach/Siloti BWV 855a, and Ligeti's 'Fanfares' (from Musica ricercata) -->

Mompou's two "Au clair de la lune" settings are the most intimate moments. The Catalan composer writes moonlight like smoking alone on a balcony — solitary, murmuring to himself. Cage's Dream (1948) is lighter still, nearly weightless, as though the keys were pressed by moonlight rather than fingers.

<!-- fact: includes Mompou's two versions of 'Au clair de la lune' and Cage's 1948 piano piece Dream -->

Scriabin's Fourth Sonata is the album's summit. Two movements, Andante into Prestissimo volando, marked "flight" — no longer moon-gazing but moon-bound, flying ecstatically toward the light.

<!-- fact: Scriabin's Piano Sonata No. 4 is included, its two movements Andante and Prestissimo volando charged with ecstatic flight -->

The digital edition adds two more: Hahn's Hivernale and Schoenberg's Op. 19 No. 1. The CD holds seventeen tracks; digital and vinyl hold nineteen.

<!-- fact: CD edition has 17 tracks; digital/vinyl editions have 19, adding Hahn's Hivernale and Schoenberg's Op. 19/1 -->

## From the Chopin Competition to DG Exclusive

Liu took the Chopin gold in 2021 and signed exclusively with DG in March 2022. His first studio album Waves (2023 — Rameau, Ravel, Alkan) won the Opus Klassik newcomer award; the second was The Seasons (2024); and this Lunaris is the third.

<!-- fact: 2021 Chopin gold; exclusive DG contract from March 2022; debut Waves (2023) won the 2024 Opus Klassik newcomer award; The Seasons (2024) followed -->

Three albums in three years, each with a concept, none repeating the last. That's rare in today's record business — most young pianists debut with a "Chopin-plus-Liszt showpieces" grab bag. Liu refuses.

<!-- fiction: assessment of his release strategy -->

## Taiwan Has Met Him

On December 12 and 13, 2024, Liu gave two recitals at Taipei's National Concert Hall — his first visit to Taiwan. If you were there, you probably remember the encores: he played them like a big kid finally allowed to play with his favorite toys.
