## Mozart Was Never a Powdered Wig in a Salon

If you think Mozart belongs strictly to polite drawing rooms with fine china and lace cuffs, this recording will smash your preconceptions with the force of a battering ram.

Greek-Russian maverick Teodor Currentzis and his cult-like orchestra musicAeterna—drilled in isolation deep in the Ural city of Perm—completed their revolutionary Da Ponte trilogy for Sony Classical with this incendiary account of *Don Giovanni*. It remains one of the most polarizing, thrilling operatic achievements of our century.

## Blood on Gut Strings and the Stone Guest’s Wrath

From the terrifying opening D minor chord of the overture, harsh harpsichord arpeggiation and visceral gut strings tear open the gates of the underworld. Currentzis employs manic dynamic contrasts and whip-crack tempi—frenzied allegros that verge on reckless abandon, contrasted with suffocatingly stretched adagios.

Tiliakos’s Giovanni is no suave romantic charmer, but an existential predator running on borrowed time. In the whirlwind Champagne Aria (*Fin ch'han dal vino*), the music careens forward like punk rock played on historic instruments. In the harrowing dinner scene, the Commendatore’s fatal knocking lands like thunderclaps on judgment day.

## Heated Debates at Wally Records

Few recordings in our racks spark such fiery arguments among visitors. Traditionalists complain of sacrilege and sensationalism, while younger listeners stand transfixed, astonished by the kinetic velocity of this theatrical juggernaut.

Love it or loathe it, indifference is impossible. Currentzis strips away decades of museum varnish, resurrecting a dangerous, wild Mozart staring directly into the abyss.
