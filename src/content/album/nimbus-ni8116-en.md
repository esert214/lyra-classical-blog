## How Many Doors Must a Mother Knock Upon?

Fable holds that if a mother who has just lost her child can obtain a button, or a single leaf, from a genuinely happy human being before the sun sets, her deceased infant will draw breath once again.

This is the timeless mythological seed behind *Picture a Day Like This*, the one-act chamber opera by Sir George Benjamin and dramatist Martin Crimp, premiered at the 2023 Festival d'Aix-en-Provence. Released on Nimbus Records, this world-premiere recording clinched the **2025 Gramophone Contemporary Award**. It is not merely an opera; it is an incisive metaphysical scalpel paring away modern society's glittering illusions of contentment.

## Seeking the Last Happy Soul on Earth

Running a taut sixty-five minutes without interruption, the dramatic tension never relents.

French mezzo-soprano Marianne Crebassa sings the devastated Mother (Woman), clutching the swaddling clothes of her lost child. She crosses thresholds into the private sanctums of seemingly blissful figures: a pair of ecstatic young lovers; an ultra-wealthy art collector surrounded by priceless antiquities; a celebrated composer lavished with global fame.

Yet each threshold reveals a hollow void. The lovers secretly slice their skin to verify that they can still feel; the collector is eaten alive by nocturnal paranoia that his treasures will be stolen; the composer is paralyzed by artistic impotence and dread of mortality. Crebassa's voice shifts from hollow despair to blinding emotional purity, buoyed by the twenty-two musicians of the Mahler Chamber Orchestra in textures that gleam with diamond-hard luminescence and icy clarity.

## Entering Zuleika's Sanctuary

The opera resolves in its haunting final scene: the mother enters a lush, hidden garden sequestered deep within the metropolis.

There, a woman named Zuleika tends to delicate blossoms. She radiates an unmistakable, transcendent serenity. Trembling, the mother approaches and begs for a single leaf from the foliage.

Yet Zuleika turns gently to deliver the truth: the garden is merely a fragile conservatory awaiting the imminent roar of urban bulldozers. Real happiness is never an exemption from suffering; it is the courage to gaze upon the cruelty of existence and still choose to love a flower that is doomed to fade.

<!-- fact: The Mahler Chamber Orchestra (MCO), founded by Claudio Abbado, premiered the work and recorded it under Benjamin's direction, continuing a historic artistic partnership -->

As delicate chime overtones dissolve into silent strings, the mother withdraws her empty hands. She gathers no miraculous leaf, yet the child's soul—and the listener's heart—finds an unearthly stillness. George Benjamin affirms that even in our fractured twenty-first century, transcendent beauty can take root in the most shattered crevices of grief.
