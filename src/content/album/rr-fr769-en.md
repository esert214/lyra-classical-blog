## A Homesick Symphony Meets a Century of Rhythm

In 1893, Dvořák wrote his New World Symphony in New York. He said it held Native American melodies and African-American spirituals—he heard Bohemian homesickness inside America's soil.

<!-- fact: Dvořák completed the Ninth Symphony in New York in 1893, incorporating African-American spiritual and Native American elements -->

More than a century later, Manfred Honeck places this most classic of symphonies beside a work by a contemporary American composer.

Carlos Simon's Four Black American Dances was premiered in 2023 by the Boston Symphony under Nelsons—and this album is its first commercial recording.

<!-- fact: Four Black American Dances was premiered by the BSO/Nelsons in 2023; this album is its world-premiere commercial release -->

This is no random pairing. One source of the New World's inspiration was Harry Burleigh, Dvořák's African-American student, singing spirituals to him. Simon's four dances grow directly from gospel music and Black American dance traditions.

<!-- fact: Harry Burleigh introduced Dvořák to African-American spirituals, influencing the New World Symphony -->

Two works, over a century apart, in conversation—two ends of one bloodline.

<!-- fiction: interpretation of the album's programming -->

## Honeck Makes the Familiar Fresh

Gramophone praises Honeck for a rare gift: making even the most worn-out works sound engagingly fresh.

<!-- fact: Gramophone praised Honeck's rare ability to make well-worn works sound fresh -->

Recorded live in Pittsburgh's Heinz Hall in February 2025, you hear that famous English horn theme of the Largo—Honeck doesn't stretch it into saccharine nostalgia; he gives every phrase the weight of breath, like a letter written home late at night in a foreign land.

<!-- fact: The recording is live from Heinz Hall, February 2025; the Largo's English horn theme is its most famous moment -->

## Four Dancing Voices

The four dances each have their own character:

"Ring Shout" opens jazzy, garish, syncopation-heavy; "Waltz" is lush enough to nod toward Ravel; "Tap!" is so playful you want to stamp along; and the finale, "Holy Dance," is a brass-blaring riot—critics describe it as Gershwin laced with Stravinsky.

<!-- fact: The movements are Ring Shout, Waltz, Tap!, Holy Dance; MusicWeb described their stylistic roots via Copland, Ravel, Gershwin, and Stravinsky -->

If you've never heard Carlos Simon, these four dances are the perfect introduction.

## The Taiwan Connection

The Pittsburgh Symphony has real history with Taiwan.

<!-- fact: The PSO performed in Taiwan during Maazel's tenure -->

Its first Taiwan visit came under Lorin Maazel. And Honeck's very first international tour as music director was the 2009 Asia tour—on May 20, 2009, he conducted the opening concert of Kaohsiung's World Games Stadium, the orchestra's Kaohsiung debut.

<!-- fact: On May 20, 2009 the PSO played the World Games Stadium opening concert in Kaohsiung—their Kaohsiung debut and Honeck's first international tour with the orchestra -->

## After August

From late August 2026, Honeck leads the orchestra on a European tour—Lucerne, the Salzburg Festival (the only American orchestra invited), the Concertgebouw in Amsterdam, Hamburg's Elbphilharmonie, Vienna's Konzerthaus. In the repertoire: this New World, and Simon's four dances.
