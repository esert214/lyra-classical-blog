## A Child Counting Constellations

In the spring of 2025, inside the storied auditorium of New York's Carnegie Hall, more than two thousand listeners held their breath as the house lights dimmed.

<!-- fact: This album preserves Yunchan Lim's live recital at Carnegie Hall in 2025, released internationally by Decca Classics in February 2026 (catalog 487 1517) -->

Onto the stage walked the slight, unassuming figure of Yunchan Lim, head bowed in quiet humility. Sitting at the Steinway, he closed his eyes and inhaled deeply. When his fingers met the keys, the serene opening Aria bloomed into the silence.

<!-- fiction: capturing Lim's characteristic reverence and modesty as he takes the stage -->

This is not Glenn Gould's surgical, kinetic detachment, nor is it Angela Hewitt's manicured British poise. Lim's Bach radiates an ancient, disarming purity: like a solitary child on a frostbitten hill at midnight, connecting the stars with a fingertip.

<!-- fiction: poetic comparison with legendary Goldberg interpretations -->

## Thirty Labors of Faith and Counterpoint

The *Goldberg Variations* are every pianist's Mount Everest: an Aria followed by thirty variations, with an exact canon embedded at every third interval, creating a monumental cathedral of mathematics and grace.

<!-- fact: The Goldberg Variations BWV 988 feature an architectural design with strict canons progressing from unison to ninth at every third variation -->

Yet Lim transforms this intricate maze into living flesh and blood. In the acrobatic hand-crossings of Variations 5, 14, and 20, his articulation is as swift and weightless as a hummingbird's wing, free of percussive violence. The right hand runs like a crystal brook while the left anchors deep, ancient roots.

<!-- fiction: depicting the velocity and polyphonic lucidity of the virtuosic variations -->

And in the fabled "Black Pearl"—Variation 25 in G minor—the universe collapses into sorrow. Lim stretches time to its breaking point, each chromatic descent aching like tears falling upon stone.

<!-- fact: Wanda Landowska christened the heartbreaking G minor 25th variation the "Black Pearl" of the work -->

## The Aria Returns: Made New by the Journey

After seventy minutes of spiritual pilgrimage, the initial Aria returns completely unaltered.

<!-- fact: Bach famously directs the Aria to be repeated in full at the conclusion (Aria da Capo e Fine), sealing the circular architecture -->

The notes are identical, but we are not. Having traversed birth, joy, revelry, and grief, we return to the quiet threshold of our beginning. The world remains cruel, yet something profound within us has been irrevocably cleansed.

<!-- fiction: philosophical realization of the transformative power of Bach's circular structure -->
