## A Song, a World

The art song (Lied) is one of the most intimate forms in classical music. It needs only a singer and a piano, yet can sing an entire world.

<!-- fact: The art song is one of the most intimate forms of classical music, centered on singer and piano -->

And this Lieder album, sung by Egyptian soprano Fatma Said, is a feast of German art songs.

<!-- fact: This is a German art song album sung by Fatma Said -->

From Schubert, the Mendelssohn siblings, to Brahms and Schumann, this album spans the golden age of the German Lied.

<!-- fact: The album covers Schubert, the Mendelssohn siblings, Brahms, and Schumann -->

## Schubert's Shepherd on the Rock

One of the album's highlights is Schubert's late masterpiece "Der Hirt auf dem Felsen" (The Shepherd on the Rock).

<!-- fact: 'Der Hirt auf dem Felsen' is a late Schubert masterpiece -->

What makes this song special is that, besides soprano and piano, it adds a clarinet—played here by the world's foremost clarinettist, Sabine Meyer.

<!-- fact: 'Der Hirt auf dem Felsen' adds a clarinet part, played by Sabine Meyer -->

Meyer's clarinet and Said's voice converse amid valley-echoing melodies, creating an atmosphere both lonely and warm.

<!-- fiction: description of the music -->

## An Egyptian Heart

Fatma Said is one of the most prominent young sopranos of recent years. Born in Cairo and trained in Berlin, she carries a voice both classical and modern.

<!-- fact: Fatma Said was born in Cairo and trained in Berlin -->

Her interpretation is praised as "colorful and agile." In the German Lied—a field long dominated by Western singers—she gives these songs a fresh, cross-cultural vitality.

<!-- opinion: commentary on Said's interpretation -->

## Why Listen

This album earned high praise from The Classic Review, hailed as one of the finest art song recordings in recent years.

<!-- fact: This album was praised by The Classic Review -->

Though the art song is "small," it is the ultimate test of a singer's skill. Said shows masterly command of text, music, and emotion—this is an album that lets you quiet down and listen closely.

<!-- opinion: subjective commentary -->

## Further Listening

If you love German art songs, explore Mendelssohn's Scottish Symphony (also on Warner). And Wagner's The Flying Dutchman shows another side of German vocal music.

<!-- fact: Mendelssohn's Scottish was recorded by the Rotterdam Philharmonic/Shani; The Flying Dutchman is on Decca -->
