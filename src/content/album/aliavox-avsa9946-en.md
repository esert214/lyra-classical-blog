## Stripping Two Centuries of Dust from the Revolutionary

We have grown accustomed to Beethoven symphonies rendered as monumental, granite colossi by modern massed symphony orchestras. Yet at age eighty, Catalan early music patriarch Jordi Savall dismantled all accrued varnish with his project *Beethoven Révolution*.

Released on his deluxe Alia Vox label, Savall leads Le Concert des Nations on period instruments, adhering rigorously to Beethoven’s original metronome markings. Utilizing gut strings, natural brass, and calfskin timpani struck with hard wooden sticks, he returns these scores to the combustible energy of the Enlightenment.

## Joy Unbound in the Ninth Symphony

Under Savall's direction, the famous Allegretto of the Seventh Symphony is not a turgid funeral shuffle but an intensely buoyant, dancing procession. In the Sixth Symphony’s storm, the snap of gut strings and the crack of wooden mallets recreate thunder so vividly you can almost smell rain-soaked clay.

In the colossal Ninth Symphony, the choral finale discards twentieth-century bombast. When the double basses introduce the *Ode to Joy* recitative, it sounds like citizens murmuring around an open plaza, exploding into brotherhood with spontaneous, uninhibited ecstasy.

I never argue. I simply set the SACD to the finale of the Seventh. The sheer velocity, rhythmic articulation, and blazing natural brass leaves them spellbound. This is not smaller Beethoven; it is Beethoven restored to his untamed revolutionary youth.
