## A Solitary Voice Behind the Iron Curtain

Written for the legendary Mstislav Rostropovich, Shostakovich’s two cello concertos represent the most dangerous, unsparing trials in twentieth-century string literature.

British cellist Sheku Kanneh-Mason embraces this monumental challenge in his most mature artistic statement to date. Partnering with Andris Nelsons and the Boston Symphony Orchestra—a team that collected four Grammy Awards for their Shostakovich symphonic cycle—this Decca release captures both the manic defiance of the First Concerto and the haunted desolation of the Second.

## Clockwork Panic and Spectral Shadows

The famous four-note motto launching Concerto No. 1 fires from Kanneh-Mason's strings like gunfire. The Boston horn and timpani hound him with bureaucratic menace, transforming Soviet totalitarian machinery into sound. In the sprawling third-movement Cadenza, Sheku builds an agonizing soliloquy of desperate intensity.

By contrast, Concerto No. 2 retreats into a shadowy twilight world. Muffled bass drum thuds and macabre xylophone clatter frame Sheku's brooding, bruised lyricism, evoking an exile wandering through frozen birch forests.

<!-- fact: Rostropovich premiered Concerto No. 1 in 1959 after memorizing it in four days; Concerto No. 2 emerged in 1966 following Shostakovich's first heart attack -->

## The Sharpest Blade on Our Shelves

Customers often ask whether classical music exists merely to relax tired nerves. I smile and hand them this disc.

Great art does not always flatter or soothe; sometimes it cuts to the bone. In Boston's golden acoustic, Sheku's cello carves open a wound of historical memory, affirming human dignity against brute tyranny.
