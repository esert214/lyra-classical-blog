## Welcome to the Twilight of Weimar Berlin

"Willkommen, bienvenue, welcome!"

When Academy Award winner Eddie Redmayne croaks those opening syllables in an uncanny, bone-chilling falsetto, the subterranean hedonism of 1930s Berlin’s Kit Kat Club bursts into raw acoustic reality.

Recorded for Decca Broadway following its Olivier Award-sweeping London run and transfer to the August Wilson Theatre on Broadway, this 2024 cast album redefines John Kander and Fred Ebb’s immortal masterpiece. This is no nostalgic escapist divertissement; it is a harrowing post-mortem on political apathy.

## A Marionette of Doom and a Fractured Sally

Redmayne’s Emcee is neither Joel Grey’s sharp satirist nor Alan Cumming’s pansexual hedonist. He is an unsettling chimera—part malevolent carnival marionette, part personification of societal decay. Through numbers like *Two Ladies* and *Money*, his vocal inflections warp between giddy childishness and venomous menace.

Opposite him, Scottish actress Gayle Rankin delivers a gut-wrenching, unvarnished Sally Bowles. When she screams the titular anthem *Cabaret*, she is not serenading customers; she is an addict choosing self-destruction while jackboots march in the streets outside.

<!-- fact: Redmayne won the Olivier Award for Best Actor in a Musical for this interpretation and earned a 2024 Tony Award nomination on Broadway -->

## Reflections in the Shop Window

Whenever the haunting acoustic reprise of *Tomorrow Belongs to Me* gives way to the chilling drum stroke and sudden silence at the close, our record shop falls dead quiet.

*Cabaret* endures because it warns us that democratic collapses do not begin with thunderclaps; they unfold while polite crowds drink champagne and pretend the cabaret will never end.
