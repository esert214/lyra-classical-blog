## This Article Was Written Before Its Album Was Born

Full disclosure first: this album comes out on September 25, 2026. As I write, it doesn't exist in a single record shop on Earth.

<!-- fact: Rubicon RCD1224 is scheduled for release on September 25, 2026; this article is a pre-release preview written before launch -->

But I couldn't wait. Because this story deserves telling early: a violinist who waited nearly half a century for one concerto.

<!-- fiction: opening framing on the theme of waiting -->

## The Painting from Age Fourteen

At fourteen (around 1974), Thomas Bowes was a dreaming teenager. He had fallen so deeply for Elgar's Violin Concerto he didn't know what to do with himself — so he picked up a paintbrush and made a picture.

<!-- fact: Bowes has said his devotion to Elgar's Violin Concerto goes back to his teens, and that around 1974 he painted a picture that later became this album's cover -->

Half a century later, that painting is the album cover. A boy waited a lifetime and finally packed his dream into a CD jewel case — with his own fourteen-year-old hand on the front. Things like this almost never happen in classical music.

<!-- fiction: reflection on the cover story -->

Bowes, born 1960, was the founding first violin of the Maggini Quartet, leader of the London Mozart Players, a veteran of 200-plus film scores — a first-class player "everyone knows but nobody can name." He didn't play Elgar seriously until his thirties; in 2006, director Ken Russell heard his concerto, declared him "The Magician," and Bowes resolved: this gets recorded if it's the last thing I do.

<!-- fact: Bowes (b. 1960), founding first violin of the Maggini Quartet (1988–93), leader of the London Mozart Players, 200+ film-score credits; took up Elgar seriously in his thirties; Ken Russell dubbed him 'The Magician' in 2006, crystallizing the recording resolve -->

## Abbey Road Studio 1: The Room Elgar Opened Himself

The sessions took place in Abbey Road Studio 1 — a room Elgar himself opened in 1931, where a year later, in 1932, he conducted sixteen-year-old Menuhin in the legendary recording of this very concerto.

<!-- fact: Abbey Road Studio 1 was opened by Elgar in 1931; in 1932 Elgar conducted the sixteen-year-old Yehudi Menuhin there in the classic recording of the B minor Violin Concerto -->

Bowes has logged thousands of hours in that room — LPO and Academy sessions in the eighties and nineties, then the Spice Girls, then film scores. He calls it a giant cello: generous, warm, occasionally treacherous, requiring a three-time Grammy winner like Arne Akselberg behind the glass to keep it in line.

<!-- fact: Bowes has recorded in the studio for decades across classical and film work; this album's engineer is three-time Grammy winner Arne Akselberg -->

The concerto was taped in December 2022; the five short pieces followed in May 2026. Same team, same room, three and a half years apart. Bowes says both sessions left everyone emotional — "like a dam bursting." Half a lifetime held back, finally let go.

<!-- fact: the concerto was recorded in December 2022, the five arrangements in May 2026, all at Abbey Road Studio 1 -->

<!-- fiction: the 'bursting dam' metaphor renders his interview account of the sessions' emotion -->

## Five Miniatures: What Elgar Never Got Around To

Beyond the concerto, the disc holds five miniatures: Salut d'amour, Chanson de matin, Chanson de nuit, La Capricieuse, Sospiri. The point: all five existed only for violin and piano. Elgar himself never orchestrated them.

<!-- fact: the five miniatures (Salut d'amour Op. 12, Chanson de matin Op. 15/2, Chanson de nuit Op. 15/1, La Capricieuse Op. 17, Sospiri Op. 70) existed only in violin-and-piano form; Elgar never made orchestral versions -->

The orchestrator is Eleanor Alberga — Bowes's wife since 1992, Jamaican-British composer, OBE. The two form the duo Double Exposure and have played these five pieces countless times in their violin-and-piano originals, knowing each other's next breath with eyes closed.

<!-- fact: arranger Eleanor Alberga OBE (b. 1949, Jamaican-British), married to Bowes since 1992; the Double Exposure duo; her two violin concertos were commissioned by and premiered for Bowes -->

What does it mean to be arranged by the person closest to you? It means she knows what these pieces weigh inside him — which notes must never be touched, where one may dare. This isn't orchestration. It's thirty years of marriage, annotated.

<!-- fiction: interpretation of the spouses' arranging relationship -->

## The Orchestra: London's Finest "Temps"

The accompaniment comes from Sinfonia Arcadiana — a "recording orchestra," which is to say London's finest film-session players, each busy elsewhere, convened for this one project. Conductor Nic Fallowfield is an old friend; Bowes studied the concerto with him back in the day.

<!-- fact: Sinfonia Arcadiana is a session-player recording orchestra assembled for this project; conductor Nicholas Fallowfield, with whom Bowes studied the concerto years ago -->

One hour twelve minutes, eight tracks, out September 25. As I finish writing, the album is still days from being born.
