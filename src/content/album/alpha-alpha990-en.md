## Sixteen Swans Soaring into the Aurora

On April 21, 1915, Jean Sibelius recorded in his diary: "Today at ten to eleven, I saw sixteen swans. God, what beauty! They circled over me for a long time. Disappeared into the solar haze like a gleaming, silver ribbon... That theme for horn and trombone..."

That mystical encounter in the Finnish wilderness birthed the soaring Swan Theme of the Fifth Symphony’s finale. Conducted by Finnish maestro Santtu-Matias Rouvali leading Sweden’s National Orchestra, the Gothenburg Symphony, this Alpha Classics release captures the raw, primeval pulse of northern nature.

## Organic Growth and Glacial Splendor

Trained as a percussionist, Rouvali brings a visceral tactile rhythmic drive to the podium. In Symphony No. 3, he reveals crisp, classical balance; in the colossal opening movement of Symphony No. 5, the transition from brooding mystery to dancing scherzo feels like volcanic spring melt beneath winter ice.

When the horn section launches into the swinging, bell-like Swan Theme in the finale, the Gothenburg strings unfurl like the northern lights across an unpolluted sky. The famous six isolated closing chords hammer down like granite pillars, separated by silence that swallows the breath.
