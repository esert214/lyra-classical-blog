## An Album Embedded with Living Wildflower Seeds

Opening the physical package of *EDEN*, you will find an insert made of seeded paper. As Joyce DiDonato writes in the foreword: "Plant this paper, water it, and watch the flowers grow. That is what music does within our hearts."

This is a breathtaking, 400-year ecological journey. DiDonato and Il Pomo d'Oro weave together Charles Ives’s metaphysical *The Unanswered Question*, Handel’s hymns to nature, Mahler’s immortal *Ich bin der Welt abhanden gekommen*, and newly commissioned works by Oscar-winning composer Rachel Portman, calling humanity to heal its relationship with the living Earth.

<!-- fact: Released in 2022 by Erato, nominated for Best Classical Solo Vocal Album at the 65th Grammy Awards, paired with a global 45-city educational tour -->

## Blossoms Blooming Between Handel and Mahler

Ives’s quiet enigma opens the journey, blooming into Handel’s beloved *Ombra mai fù* from *Serse*—a hymn of heartfelt gratitude to the sheltering shade of a plane tree.

In Mahler's *Ich bin der Welt abhanden gekommen*, period gut strings lend an austere, ethereal lightness to Mahler's late-Romantic sorrow. DiDonato’s floating pianissimo on the final line—"I live alone in my heaven, in my love, in my song"—is mesmerizing in its tranquility.

<!-- fact: In every tour stop across five continents, DiDonato organized local environmental tree-planting initiatives with schoolchildren -->

## Tending the Garden Within

We do not need to flee modern reality to find paradise. As long as we cultivate humility and gratitude, the garden of Eden blooms within our very breathing.
