## What Would a Mother Do

Jenůfa is one of Janáček's most famous operas, based on a true tragedy in a Moravian village.

<!-- fact: Jenůfa is based on a real tragedy in a Moravian village -->

The story revolves around the young Jenůfa, her cousins Laca and Števa, and her stern, strong stepmother, the Kostelnička.

<!-- fact: The story revolves around Jenůfa, Laca, Števa, and the stepmother Kostelnička -->

But at the heart of the opera is a heartbreaking question: how far will a mother go to protect her child?

<!-- fiction: question about the opera's core -->

To preserve the family's honor, the Kostelnička does something irreversible—and Janáček, with his music, gives this moral abyss a chilling humanity.

<!-- fact: The Kostelnička does something irreversible to preserve honor -->

## Janáček's Voice

Janáček's musical language is unique. He argued that operatic vocal lines should be "like speech"—capturing the natural rhythm and inflection of the Czech language.

<!-- fact: Janáček argued operatic vocals should be speech-like, capturing Czech rhythm -->

This makes his operas both raw and intimate, filled with a real, almost cruel emotion.

<!-- opinion: commentary on Janáček's style -->

Rattle's recording with the London Symphony Orchestra renders Janáček's unique orchestral writing—woven with Moravian folk color and modern harmony—with exquisite precision.

<!-- fiction: commentary on Rattle's interpretation -->

## That Famous Farewell

The ending of Act III of Jenůfa is one of the most moving moments in opera history.

<!-- fiction: commentary -->

When all the crimes are revealed, when all the pain reaches its peak, Jenůfa chooses forgiveness. She reaches out to Laca and speaks that famous line: "We have to live."

<!-- fact: The opera ends with Jenůfa's forgiveness; the famous line is 'We have to live' -->

Swedish soprano Agneta Eichenholz's singing here makes this ending both heavy and full of the hope of rebirth.

<!-- fiction: commentary on the singing -->

## Why Listen

This recording was released by LSO Live in 2025, an important installment in Rattle's Janáček opera series.

<!-- fact: LSO Live LSO0897, released 2025, part of Rattle's Janáček series -->

Rattle's affinity with Janáček runs deep; he has a unique understanding of this "speech-like" musical language. Combined with world-class LSO playing, this is a recording that lets you enter Janáček's world completely.

<!-- opinion: subjective recommendation -->
