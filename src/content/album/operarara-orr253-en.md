## A Diva Offering Her Soul on the Altar

As *The Guardian* memorably put it: Ermonela Jaho doesn’t merely act; she tears open her heart on stage and presents it to the audience.

This tour-de-force debut solo recital with Opera Rara, *Anima Rara*, pays homage to legendary Italian soprano Rosina Storchio (1872–1922), the original creator of Cio-Cio-San in Puccini's *Madama Butterfly*. The program gathers forgotten and celebrated verismo gems by Mascagni (*Iris*), Giordano (*Fedora*), and Leoncavallo (*La bohème*), every syllable saturated in raw emotional truth.

<!-- fact: Released in 2020 by Opera Rara, this album won the Vocal Recital category at the International Classical Music Awards (ICMA) -->

## Fragile Pianissimos and Bleeding Accents

In Mascagni’s *Iris*, Jaho captures the tragic innocence of a victimized soul with an ethereal, spun-silver pianissimo suspended in mid-air. Her voice refuses cold, sterile polish; instead, it vibrates with human vulnerability that cuts straight to the marrow.

Conductor Andrea Battistoni and the Orquestra de la Comunitat Valenciana provide tempestuous, warm underpinnings. In Catalani’s *La Wally*, her soaring despair proves what genuine verismo signifies: not fairy-tale royalty, but real human hearts bleeding in the cold mud of reality.

<!-- fact: Rosina Storchio was the supreme lyric verismo soprano of her era, creating the title role of Madama Butterfly at La Scala in 1904 -->

## An Empty Hall After the Final Bow

Finishing this recording leaves you spiritually cleansed yet physically exhausted. In an era dominated by digital autotune and curated perfection, Jaho’s searing authenticity is a stark reminder: supreme art requires fearless vulnerability.
