## The Fiery Heart of a Young Titan

The fateful day a nineteen-year-old Johannes Brahms knocked on Robert Schumann’s door in Düsseldorf, the course of music history altered forever. Schumann famously wrote "Genius" in his diary, proclaiming to the world in *Neue Bahnen* the arrival of a new musical prophet. The music of this young Brahms was not the melancholic autumn mist of his twilight years, but a furious, surging fire of youth.

French phenom Alexandre Kantorow chooses for this breathtaking BIS release Brahms’s early volcanic outpourings: the *Piano Sonata No. 2, Op. 2*, the four *Ballades, Op. 10*, and the later *Rhapsodies, Op. 79*. In Kantorow’s hands, the piano ceases to be a mere wooden percussive mechanism; it becomes a live volcano on the verge of eruption.

## Epic Poems Carved into the Keyboard

Listen to Kantorow navigate the opening Ballade *Edward*, inspired by the blood-drenched Scottish ballad of patricide. The ominous low octaves sound like heavy, guilt-laden footsteps in the mud; suddenly, the middle section unleashes an arresting torrent of triplets. Kantorow possesses an extraordinary dynamic reserve, keeping massive fortissimo chords resonant and transparent without a shred of harshness.

Yet in the Andante con espressione of the Second Sonata, he retracts the tempest instantly. Caressing the keys with tender vulnerability, he sings the German folk tune *Mir ist leide* like an antique love letter hidden in an attic trunk, stained with long-forgotten tears.

<!-- fact: Brahms composed his Ballades Op. 10 in 1854, deeply influenced by Herder's Scottish folk poetry translations; his Sonata Op. 2 was written before he turned twenty -->

## Midnight Reflections Under the Turntable Lamp

After closing the shop doors at midnight, I cue up this recording. Rain lashes against the storefront glass, and Kantorow’s chords surge in the shadows like an oceanic tide.

We often assume that growing older means learning resignation. But Brahms reminds us that true maturity means harboring an unquenchable fire of conviction within, even after witnessing the world's sorrows. As long as these notes ring out, that youth never dies.
