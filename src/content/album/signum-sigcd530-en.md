## Echoes of the 1913 Paris Riot

On May 29, 1913, at the Théâtre des Champs-Élysées, the premiere of Stravinsky’s *The Rite of Spring* ignited the most infamous riot in music history. With a single battering downbeat, the polite salon aesthetics of the 19th century shattered into irrecoverable dust.

A century later, Finnish master Esa-Pekka Salonen and the Philharmonia Orchestra reclaim this mountain peak. Viewing the score with a master composer's crystalline intellect, Salonen turns this primal ritual into both a bone-crushing sensory assault and a breathtaking triumph of structural precision.

## Primitive Eruptions from Frozen Soil

The opening high-register bassoon solo emerges like a prehistoric fern forcing its way through permafrost—bleak, unadorned, and terrifyingly alive. Then the iconic polychords of *Augurs of Spring* fall like giant flint stone axes striking wet soil, the Philharmonia strings biting with surgical velocity.

In the climactic *Sacrificial Dance*, orchestra and conductor navigate frantic meter shifts (5/16, 3/16, 2/16) with staggering athleticism. The bass drum blows and tam-tam crashes scorch the ears with primeval ferocity.

## Shattering Civilized Illusions

Living in temperature-controlled apartments and glassy offices, we forget the elemental savagery that birthed our world. *The Rite of Spring* is the lightning bolt that incinerates complacency, forcing us to stand barefoot on scorched earth and listen to our pulses thundering with the planet.
