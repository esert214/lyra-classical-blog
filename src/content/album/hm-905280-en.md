## Peeling Back the Curtain to Paris, 1928

We have all heard Ravel's *Boléro* countless times, but have you ever heard the true acoustic colors of its 1928 Paris premiere?

François-Xavier Roth and Les Siècles perform on authentic French period instruments from the dawn of the 20th century: narrow-bore French trombones, gut-stringed violins, and slender wooden bassoons. Shedding decades of thick, modern symphonic gloss, Ravel’s watchmaker precision and exotic palette sparkle with startling transparency.

## Wit and Clockwork Intrigue in Toledo

The centerpiece of the album is the one-act opera *L'Heure espagnole*, a dazzling display of orchestral sleight-of-hand. Set inside a clockmaker’s shop in Toledo, pendulum ticks, ticking springs, and automaton chimes dance through the orchestra pit.

Mezzo-soprano Isabelle Druet embodies the mischievous Concepción with playful Gallic charm. The period French flutes and oboes deliver a woody, nasal sweetness that modern instruments simply cannot recreate.

<!-- fact: Ravel's L'Heure espagnole premiered in 1911 at the Opéra-Comique in Paris, blending clockwork motifs with fiery Spanish rhythms -->

## The Hypnotic Crescendo of the Machine

In this *Boléro*, the side drum enters at the very threshold of audibility. As each period instrument steps into the hypnotic melody, their grainy individual voices weave an exotic acoustic tapestry. When the climax collapses into dissonant destruction, it feels like an exquisite automaton engine burning out in glorious heat.
