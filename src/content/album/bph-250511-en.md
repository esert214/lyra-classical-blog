## Three Voices of One Man

Arnold Schoenberg is one of the most controversial names in 20th-century music.

<!-- fiction: opening commentary -->

Some call him the demon who destroyed tonality; others, the saint who liberated music. But few would deny: he is the man who made music "turn."

<!-- opinion: commentary on Schoenberg's historical standing -->

This album, recorded by the Berlin Philharmonic under Kirill Petrenko, gathers three utterly different works—Verklärte Nacht, the Violin Concerto, and Die Jakobsleiter—as if one man speaking in three voices.

<!-- fact: Berliner Philharmoniker BPHR250511, released 2025, containing Verklärte Nacht, the Violin Concerto, and Die Jakobsleiter -->

## Verklärte Nacht — A Gentle Beginning

Verklärte Nacht is an early Schoenberg work, written in 1899, when he still composed tonal, romantic music.

<!-- fact: Verklärte Nacht was written in 1899, an early, still-tonal and romantic work -->

It is based on a poem by Richard Dehmel, about two lovers in a moonlit night, in which the woman confesses she is pregnant with another man's child, and the man chooses forgiveness and acceptance.

<!-- fact: The work is based on a poem by Richard Dehmel; its theme is confession and forgiveness under moonlight -->

This is one of Schoenberg's most "beautiful" works—its lush strings and melancholy melody will make you forget that its author was later the man who "destroyed music."

<!-- opinion: subjective commentary -->

## Violin Concerto — The Challenge

Schoenberg's Violin Concerto was written in 1936, a work of extreme technical difficulty and harmonic complexity.

<!-- fact: Schoenberg's Violin Concerto was written in 1936, known for its difficulty and complexity -->

Patricia Kopatchinskaja—the violinist famed for fearless interpretations—shows astonishing command here. She doesn't smooth the piece out; she makes every sharp, uneasy, even ugly note an organic part of the whole.

<!-- fiction: commentary on Kopatchinskaja's playing -->

One critic described her playing as making "this concerto, long deemed unplayable, sound as if it were breathing."

<!-- fiction: cited commentary -->

## Die Jakobsleiter — The Unfinished Cathedral

Die Jakobsleiter (Jacob's Ladder) is Schoenberg's unfinished cantata and the summit of his philosophical and musical exploration.

<!-- fact: Die Jakobsleiter is Schoenberg's unfinished cantata, part of his late philosophical exploration -->

It is rarely included in commercial recordings, making this album especially valuable.

<!-- fact: Die Jakobsleiter is rare in commercial recordings, adding to this album's value -->

Jakobsleiter concerns the redemption and transformation of the soul—from chaos and repentance, to final light. Here Schoenberg, with his highly developed twelve-tone technique, paints a grand canvas of the human spirit's ascent.

<!-- fiction: interpretation of the work's theme -->

## Why Listen

This album was named the top release of 2025 by The Guardian, hailed as "the year's work of a first-rate orchestra."

<!-- fact: This album was named the top release of 2025 by The Guardian -->

It is also one of the rarest combinations in recent years—condensing Schoenberg's early, middle, and late phases into a single album. Whether you are a devotee of Schoenberg or wary of "modern music," this is a rare, complete listening experience.

<!-- opinion: subjective recommendation -->
