## Ghosts Trapped in 300-Year-Old Spruce

Imagine gathering twelve of the rarest violins ever crafted by Antonio Stradivari—with an aggregate insurance value exceeding two hundred million dollars—under one roof in London for a single recording session. It was an unprecedented logistical nightmare, and an even more daunting artistic trial.

Dutch virtuosa Janine Jansen accepted the challenge. Partnering with maestro Antonio Pappano at the piano, Jansen pairs each storied instrument—formerly played by the likes of Fritz Kreisler, Jascha Heifetz, and Nathan Milstein—with a piece perfectly calibrated to awaken its unique soul.

<!-- fact: Organized by renowned violin dealers J&A Beare, recorded at Cadogan Hall in London and documented in the film 'Falling for Stradivari' -->

## Twelve Living, Breathing Creatures

A Stradivarius is never an obedient tool. As Jansen noted during the intensive sessions: "Each violin has an uncompromising temperament. You cannot force your sound upon it; you must yield to its resonance, breathe with its wood."

Listen to the 1714 "ex-Joachim-Ma" in Brahms's *Violin Sonata No. 2*, dark and intoxicating like aged cognac. Switch to the 1734 "Kreisler", and the upper register glistens like molten amber in Kreisler’s own salon gems. Pappano’s sensitive touch at the Steinway breathes like an operatic conductor cradling a singer.

<!-- fact: Repertoire spans masterpieces by Brahms, Clara and Robert Schumann, Suk, Tchaikovsky, Falla, and Kreisler -->

## Fingerprints Left on Eternity

Centuries pass, and the titan hands that once gripped these fingerboards have long returned to dust. Yet the cellular vibrations in the varnish and spruce remain perpetually youthful. We do not own these instruments—we are merely their fleeting caretakers.
