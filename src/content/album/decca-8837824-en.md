## A Bear, a Jar of Marmalade

When was the last time you saw a musical that made you laugh all the way through—and quietly moved you to tears?

<!-- fiction: opening question -->

In winter 2025, London's West End's Savoy Theatre welcomed a small Peruvian bear in a blue coat and red hat—Paddington. With him came that ever-present jar of orange marmalade, and a story about where "home" is.

## A Bear Who Sings

Turning a bear into a singing protagonist sounds risky. But Tom Fletcher—lead singer of McFly—pulled it off.

<!-- fact: The musical's music and lyrics are by Tom Fletcher -->

Fletcher's melodies are catchy, carrying the wit and warmth of British musical theatre. This isn't an abstruse work of art; it's genuinely "family" music you can hum after one listen.

<!-- fiction: subjective description of the musical style -->

When James Hameed's voice rises from the depths of the stage, speaking that first line—"Please look after this bear"—everyone's heart melts.

## The Definition of Home

The most moving thing about Paddington has never been the bear's adventures, but the question he keeps asking: what is home?

<!-- fiction: thematic interpretation -->

Paddington comes from "darkest Peru." He loses his home, yet finds a new belonging with the Brown family in London. This is a story about immigration, about being accepted, about "home being not a place, but the people who make you feel safe."

<!-- fact: Paddington comes from Peru and is taken in by the Brown family; the story centers on belonging and acceptance -->

In today's world, that means more than ever.

<!-- fiction: commentary on contemporary relevance -->
