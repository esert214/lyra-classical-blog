## A Historic Reckoning Half a Century Delayed

"I am a survivor of the 20th-century Holocaust; every note I write is a duty to those who fell behind the barbed wire and cannot speak." — Mieczysław Weinberg.

Weinberg's entire family perished in Nazi concentration camps; after fleeing on foot to the Soviet Union, he suffered further persecution under Stalin's anti-Semitic purges. Though revered by Shostakovich as a brother in arms, his 22 symphonies lay largely forgotten. That changed in 2019 when Lithuanian conductor Mirga Gražinytė-Tyla, the City of Birmingham Symphony Orchestra, and Gidon Kremer recorded this earth-shaking release for DG, sweeping **Gramophone Recording of the Year**.

<!-- fact: Released by Deutsche Grammophon in 2019 to mark Weinberg's centenary, winning both Orchestral and Recording of the Year at the Gramophone Awards -->

## An Epic Kaddish for the Warsaw Ghetto

The centerpiece is Weinberg's 1991 *Symphony No. 21 'Kaddish'*, dedicated to the victims of the Warsaw Ghetto.

Spanning nearly an hour in six continuous sections, Gidon Kremer’s violin solo wanders like a lonely ghost across the smoking ruins of Treblinka. Suddenly, the orchestral percussion explodes like heavy panzers crushing concrete. In the devastating final pages, Mirga herself wordlessly hums a lullaby, like a grief-stricken mother rocking a lost child above a mass grave.

<!-- fact: Weinberg’s 21st Symphony weaves quotations from Shostakovich's final Viola Sonata and Mahler's Fourth Symphony -->

## Bearing Witness for the Silenced

This is not easy listening; it is a sacred monument carved from tears and memory. It forces us to confront humanity's darkest chapter while sheltering that fragile, stubborn flame of human dignity.
