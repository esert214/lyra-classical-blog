## A New Regime Burns the Whole Solar System First

In September 2024, Pappano officially took up the baton as Chief Conductor of the London Symphony Orchestra.

<!-- fact: Pappano became Chief Conductor of the LSO in September 2024 -->

After twenty-two years in an opera house, a man finally has an orchestra of his own. His first move? The Planets. The message is unmistakable: where I'm taking you is bigger than any opera stage — big enough to hold the entire solar system.

<!-- fiction: reading the program choice as a new music director's statement -->

Released on LSO Live in March 2026, the disc captures two 2024 Barbican live concerts: the Planets from September 12, Tintagel from December 15. High-resolution DSD recording, sixty-nine minutes total.

<!-- fact: LSO Live LSO0904, released March 20, 2026; the Planets recorded September 12, 2024, Tintagel December 15, 2024, live at London's Barbican, DSD recording, about 69 minutes -->

## Mars: Scared into Your Seat First

When that grinding 5/4 ostinato of "Mars, the Bringer of War" kicks in, I sank back into my chair.

<!-- fiction: personal listening reaction -->

Pappano's Mars is in no hurry. He lets the timpani pile up blow by blow, like an army marching in from the horizon — you can see the dust, but not yet the men. The most terrifying thing is always what hasn't happened yet.

<!-- fiction: literary description of the Mars interpretation -->

This is the LSO's fourth Planets on record: Holst himself conducted one, then Previn, then Colin Davis. With three mountains in the way, Pappano deliberately takes a slower, darker road.

<!-- fact: this is the LSO's fourth commercial Planets recording, following versions by Holst himself, Previn, and Colin Davis -->

## Venus and Mercury: The Opera Man Does Lyrical Differently

Twenty-two years at the Royal Opera House gives a conductor a different touch with things light and beautiful. The strings in "Venus" and "Mercury" lie like velvet; the flute lines spin so fine they threaten to break but never do — that's an ear trained by nightly Puccini sopranos.

<!-- fiction: connecting operatic tenure with the lyrical movements' interpretation -->

In the great hymn at the heart of "Jupiter," Pappano doesn't push or milk it — he simply lets the brass blaze up on its own. That's when you understand: some conductors push the music toward you; Pappano stands behind the music and lets it walk to you by itself.

<!-- fiction: interpretive description of the Jupiter movement -->

## Neptune: The Vanishing Chorus

The wordless women's chorus closing "Neptune" is the suite's most magical stroke: voices singing offstage, growing softer and softer, until the final note doesn't end but "disappears" — the score instructs them to keep singing until nothing can be heard.

<!-- fact: 'Neptune' closes with an offstage wordless women's chorus fading to inaudibility, as specified in Holst's score -->

Here it's sung by Tenebrae. On the recording you can hear the voices being eaten by the darkness one bite at a time, like an outgoing tide. Afterward there are seconds when you don't dare breathe, for fear of waking the silence.

<!-- fact: the Neptune chorus on this recording is sung by Tenebrae -->

<!-- fiction: literary description of the listening experience -->

## Second Half: From Space Back to the Sea

Many listeners won't know Bax's Tintagel. It depicts the cliff-top castle in Cornwall of Arthurian legend, waves beating below for thousands of years.

<!-- fact: Bax's Tintagel (1917–1919) is a tone poem inspired by the Cornish Tintagel castle of Arthurian legend and its seascape -->

Fresh back from Neptune in the first half, you dive straight into Atlantic surf in the second — a brilliant piece of programming. Both are water: one imagined (outer space), one real (earthly). With a single concert, Pappano shows you how vast the sea inside British composers really is.

<!-- fiction: interpretation of the program's logic -->

## Taiwan Knows Him

Pappano came to Taipei on November 20, 2018, with Santa Cecilia and pianist Trifonov. Plenty of people still remember that night's Tchaikovsky Fourth.

<!-- fact: Pappano performed at Taipei's National Concert Hall on November 20, 2018 with Santa Cecilia, joined by Trifonov, including Tchaikovsky's Fourth Symphony -->

At the end of September he takes the LSO to Japan (Kyoto, Tokyo, Osaka, September 26–30). A Taiwan return — who knows when. But at least the Barbican nights on this disc are only one record away.
