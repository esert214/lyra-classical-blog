## Stripping the Sugarcoat from Midnight Passion

Mention *Nessun dorma*, and Pavarotti’s sun-drenched, triumphant high-B inevitably springs to mind. Yet in Puccini’s *Turandot*, Prince Calaf sings under the shadow of state executions, risking his life in total darkness before dawn.

German tenor superstar Jonas Kaufmann and maestro Antonio Pappano strip away theatrical artifice. Kaufmann’s smoldering, baritonal timbre lends Calaf the psychological complexity of a desperate gambler betting everything on love.

## Passion and Mortality in the Roman Shadows

The album surveys Puccini’s greatest tenor moments from *Manon Lescaut* and *La bohème* to *Tosca*. In *E lucevan le stelle*, Kaufmann starts with a devastated, breathless whisper on the scaffold, mourning lost touches, before opening into a searing flood of desperate despair.

Antonio Pappano draws searing passion from the Santa Cecilia Orchestra. The strings surge like the Mediterranean sea, cradling Kaufmann's volcanic clarion top notes.

<!-- fact: Puccini composed twelve operas before his death in 1924, leaving Turandot to be completed by Franco Alfano -->

## Defying Darkness Until the Dawn

We have all endured nights where dawn feels impossible. When Kaufmann holds that final defiant *Vincerò!*, it is not cheap bravado, but a bloodied fighter claiming victory against impossible odds.
