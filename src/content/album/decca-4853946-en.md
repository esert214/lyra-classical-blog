## A Young Titan Claims the Mahlerian Crown

At just twenty-eight years old, having secured the helm of both the Royal Concertgebouw Orchestra and the Chicago Symphony Orchestra, Finnish phenomenon Klaus Mäkelä is reshaping the symphonic landscape.

Following their acclaimed Sibelius cycle, Mäkelä and his Oslo Philharmonic entered the Mahlerian arena with the Fifth Symphony—a grueling journey from funeral gloom to radiant joy. Engineered with stunning dynamic headroom by Decca, this release captures an orchestra performing at white-hot intensity.

## The Adagietto: A Serenade Floating on Nordic Fjords

Opening with a chilling solo trumpet fanfare, Mahler's Fifth plunges into funeral terror and existential torment before resolving into the sublime Adagietto—scored exclusively for strings and harp as a wordless love letter to his bride, Alma.

Trained as an elite cellist, Mäkelä possesses an innate instinct for string inflection and bow speed. Refusing to drag the Adagietto into a sluggish dirge, he maintains a singing, tender tempo that flows like morning mist over an icy fjord. When the harp ripples beneath the violins, time itself seems suspended.

<!-- fact: In 2024, Mäkelä made historic headlines by becoming Music Director Designate of both the Royal Concertgebouw Orchestra (taking over in 2027) and the Chicago Symphony Orchestra -->

## The Sound of Generational Renewal

Cynics often scoff that youth cannot comprehend Mahler's neurotic despair. Mäkelä's performance dismantles that doubt in the first ten bars.

His Mahler is not the tired sigh of an old man, but the furious, open-hearted combat of a vibrant spirit grappling with fate. From funeral ashes to the blazing brass chorale of the finale, this recording crackles with life.
