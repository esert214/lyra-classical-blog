## The VHS Tape from 1987, on Broadway in 2026

If you're my age, your family definitely owned a VHS of The Lost Boys (1987).

<!-- fiction: opening with generational memory -->

Directed by Joel Schumacher: vampire town Santa Carla, two teenage brothers moving in with their grandpa, the elder seduced by a cool vampire gang, the younger teaming up with two comic-book nerds to save him. Teen movies of that era were thirty percent horror and seventy percent motorcycles, earrings, beach concerts, and "what does growing up even mean."

<!-- fact: the musical adapts Joel Schumacher's 1987 Warner film The Lost Boys, centered on the Emerson family and the Santa Carla vampire gang -->

On April 26, 2026, that VHS tape officially opened at Broadway's Palace Theatre. Previews began March 27, word of mouth built all spring, and Atlantic released the cast recording on July 24.

<!-- fact: previews began March 27, 2026, with the official opening April 26, 2026 at the Palace Theatre; the original cast recording was released July 24, 2026 by Atlantic, 28 tracks -->

## Who Wrote It? One Correction First

Some corners of the internet credit Dave Bassett or Liz Phair — there is no such connection. Stop spreading it.

<!-- fact: Dave Bassett/Liz Phair have no involvement with this show; circulating claims are false; the actual songwriters are The Rescues trio -->

The real songwriters are The Rescues: Kyler England, Adrianne "AG" Gonzalez, and Gabriel Mann, a pop-rock trio. The book is by David Hornsby (yes, from It's Always Sunny) and Chris Hoch, directed by Michael Arden — the same Michael Arden who carried Maybe Happy Ending to Tony glory.

<!-- fact: music and lyrics by The Rescues (Kyler England/Adrianne Gonzalez/Gabriel Mann); book by David Hornsby & Chris Hoch; directed by Michael Arden (also director of Maybe Happy Ending) -->

That lineup dictates the album's entire sound: not traditional Broadway brass blasts, but guitars, synths, and stacked harmonies — pop-rock with a musical-theater skeleton showing through. When lead single "Secret Comes Out" dropped at the end of May, the top comment read: "This is for those of us who grew up."

<!-- fact: lead single 'Secret Comes Out' was released May 29, 2026; the full album holds 28 tracks including bonus track 'Brother' -->

## Shoshana Bean: A Tony Twenty Years in the Making

Who is Shoshana Bean? If you've seen Broadway's Wicked, she was the first standby ever promoted to full-time Elphaba; Hell's Kitchen and Mr. Saturday Night each earned her a Tony nomination, but the trophy never came home.

<!-- fact: Bean was Broadway's first standby-to-principal Elphaba in Wicked; Tony-nominated for Hell's Kitchen and Mr. Saturday Night -->

Here she plays mother Lucy Emerson — a woman fleeing domestic violence with two sons, starting over in a strange town. Her numbers show off nothing; they're all sung the way a mother cooks dinner while swallowing her tears. The 2026 Tony for Best Featured Actress was her first, and she cried too hard to speak that night.

<!-- fact: Bean won the 2026 Tony for Best Featured Actress in a Musical as Lucy Emerson, her first Tony Award -->

## The Vampire King Was Played by Orpheus

Even better is the featured actor. Ali Louis Bourzgui won the Tony as David the vampire king — and just a year earlier, he was Orpheus in Hadestown, the innocent boy who descends into hell for love.

<!-- fact: Bourzgui won the 2026 Tony for Best Featured Actor in a Musical as David; he played Orpheus in Hadestown in 2025; Broadway debut as Tommy in The Who's Tommy (2024) -->

From Orpheus to vampire, from "I'd go to hell for you" to "welcome to hell" — the transformation is a drama in itself. Listen to his voice on the recording: bright tenor as Orpheus, lowered, dirtied, smiling as David. Same man, two kinds of eternity.

<!-- fiction: contrasting the vocal characterizations of his two roles -->

## Why Vampire Stories Always Work

Every generation of teenagers needs a vampire story. In 1987 it was AIDS and Cold War dread; in 2026 it's social media and climate anxiety — different names, same shape of fear: adults can't be trusted, the world is breaking, only your friends are real.

<!-- fiction: commentary on the cross-generational resonance of vampire tales -->

Among the recording's 28 tracks, the mother-son duets and the finale chorus deserve the most repeats. The vampires die, but nobody truly wins — that's growing up: you slay the monster, then realize you're turning into a different one.

<!-- fiction: literary interpretation of the show's themes -->

The Palace is still playing it, open run, booking past September. If New York is out of reach, start with this Atlantic recording. Press play and the Santa Carla sea breeze blows in — mind your neck.
