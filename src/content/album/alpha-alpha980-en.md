## Stripping Away Orientalist Illusions

For over a century, Cio-Cio-San has too often been reduced to a fragile, submissive porcelain doll crushed under the boot of imperialist betrayal.

Lithuanian soprano Asmik Grigorian completely shatters that caricature. In this explosive Alpha Classics release from autumn 2024, her Butterfly is not a passive victim but a woman of ferocious will, spiritual nobility, and terrifying psychological resolve.

<!-- fact: Released by Alpha Classics (ALPHA980) as a 2-CD set, this studio recording captures Grigorian and tenor sensation Jonathan Tetelman with the Polish National Radio Symphony Orchestra in Katowice -->

## Sun-Drenched Ardor and the Cold Edge of the Dagger

Partnering her as Pinkerton is Chilean-American tenor Jonathan Tetelman, whose sun-drenched Italianate top notes ring with intoxicating sensuality. In their expansive Act I love duet, *Viene la sera*, his youthful lyricism clashes and merges with Grigorian's blazing, laser-focused tone in thrilling erotic communion.

When arriving at the famous *Un bel dì, vedremo*, Grigorian avoids weeping clichés. She builds her hope like a fortress of unyielding conviction. By the catastrophic final scene (*Con onor muore*), her voice shreds the air with visceral, tragic terror as she chooses an honorable death over degradation.

<!-- fact: Grigorian achieved international stardom with her electrifying Salome at the Salzburg Festival, cementing her status as the leading dramatic soprano of her generation -->

## Midnight Requiem at Wally Records

When the final crashing G minor chord drops like an axe, silence envelopes our shop.

Puccini's genius—and his cruelty—lies in crafting the sweetest melodies imaginable only to inflict the most ruthless wounds. Grigorian does not simply sing Cio-Cio-San; she lives and bleeds her onto the magnetic tape.
