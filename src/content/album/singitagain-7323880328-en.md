## At the Gate to the Underworld Stands a Blues Singer

Hadestown's story is old as storytelling itself: Orpheus plays, the underworld shakes, Eurydice looks back. But American singer-songwriter Anaïs Mitchell rebuilt that Greek myth into a gospel of jazz and folk.

<!-- fact: Drawn from the Greek myth of Orpheus and Eurydice; the show opened at Broadway's Walter Kerr Theatre on April 17, 2019 -->

This is not a museum piece. It's the most rebellious thing the word "musical" has allowed for a long time: it sings hell into an industrial city that needs workers, in gospel, blues, and doom jazz.

<!-- fiction: describing the show's genre blend -->

<!-- fact: The scoring mixes jazz brass and folk guitar; critics broadly describe a unique voice fusing folk, blues, jazz, and gospel -->

## One Song, Two Lives

In 2006 Mitchell first planted the seed of the title song on a folk album; in 2010 she recorded the whole "folk opera" concept album Hadestown (released in the US); then in 2019 it finally grew into its full Broadway shape.

<!-- fact: The song appeared on Anaïs Mitchell's 2006 album The Brightness; the concept album Hadestown came in 2010 (produced by Ani DiFranco, featuring Justin Vernon among others); the original cast recording arrived Nov 2019 -->

This cast recording simply puts into your headphones the exact stage that — in 2019 — Taipei still couldn't see.

<!-- fiction: linking the recording experience to an audience that had no live access -->

## The Night of the Tony Landslide

Across 2019–20, Hadestown hit a once-in-a-generation run: 14 Tony nominations, 8 wins (including Best Musical, Best Original Score), then the 2020 Grammy for Best Musical Theater Album.

<!-- fact: 2019 Tony Awards: 14 nominations, 8 wins including Best Musical, Best Original Score (Anaïs Mitchell), Best Direction (Rachel Chavkin), Best Featured Actor (André De Shields); 2020 Grammy for Best Musical Theater Album -->

That searing city of the dead swept every award on both sides of the Atlantic — yet its ember is a very personal, very folk, very small one, like Eva Noblezada's Eurydice: not waiting to be saved, but finding her own way in the dark.

<!-- fiction: poetic contrast of folk roots vs. Broadway scale -->

<!-- fact: Eva Noblezada's jazz-tinted Eurydice won near-universal critical praise -->

## Taiwan Can't See It (Yet)

As of 2026 Hadestown has never appeared on a Taiwanese stage — North American tours, the West End, and Australia all ran, but no Asian production ever materialized. From 2026 a UK/Ireland tour begins and a stage-adaptation film lands in July — for Taiwanese fans, this CD is the soonest way to gather around its fire.

## Further Listening

If you love musicals telling myths of their time: Hamilton (2015) rewrote the founding of America in hip-hop; Come From Away proved, with the real 2001 story of a small Newfoundland town that sheltered stranded travelers, that "kindness" is the most moving subject a musical can have. Three shows, three utterly different sounds — and all prove a musical can be both commercial and deep.

<!-- fact: Hamilton opened in 2015; Come From Away is based on the true 2001 story of Gander, Newfoundland sheltering grounded passengers; all three are phenomenon musicals of 2010–2020 -->
