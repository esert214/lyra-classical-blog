## The Monumental Grand Opera for Paris

Among Giuseppe Verdi’s vast theatrical output, *Don Carlos* stands as his most colossal, morally complex achievement. Adapting Friedrich Schiller’s drama, Verdi wove statecraft, the terrifying machinery of the Spanish Inquisition, filial conflict, and doomed romantic passion into an inescapable web.

This landmark release documents the definitive five-act original French version conceived for the Paris Opéra in 1867. Conducted with peerless theatrical flair by Sir Antonio Pappano, and boasting an unmatched cast starring Jonas Kaufmann, Sonya Yoncheva, and Ludovic Tézier, it represents the apex of modern Verdi interpretation.

## The Sacred Bond of Brotherhood and Royal Tragedy

The subtle inflections of the French language lend Verdi’s dramatic line a courtly elegance and shadowed psychological vulnerability absent in the later Italian translation. Kaufmann’s Infante aches with melancholic torment, balancing youthful ardor with claustrophobic despair under King Philip’s oppressive gaze.

The emotional zenith arrives in the legendary friendship duet, *Dieu, tu semas dans nos âmes*, where Kaufmann and Tézier blend their golden timbres in thrilling communion, elevating brotherhood and liberty above the machinations of tyrants.
