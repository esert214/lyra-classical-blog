## Karajan's Score-Free Philosophy

Someone once asked Herbert von Karajan why he never looked at the score while conducting. His answer: "The score is in my head, the music is in my heart."

<!-- fact: Karajan was famous for conducting from memory, often with eyes closed, emphasizing that the score lives in the mind -->

Debussy and Ravel occupied two of the softest corners of his heart. This album — La Mer, Prélude à l'après-midi d'un faune, Daphnis et Chloé Suite No. 2, and Pavane — was recorded in December 1985 in the Berlin Philharmonie. You can picture Karajan with eyes shut, letting that sea rise and fall before him.

<!-- fact: Recorded Dec 1985 in the Berlin Philharmonie; producer Michel Glotz; balance engineer Günter Hermanns; released 1986 by DG -->

## The Dawn, and All Its Gradations

The Daphnis et Chloé Suite No. 2 opens with "Lever du jour": light from flute and harp climbs out of stillness, then the full orchestra like a sun finally pushing over the ridge. Karajan lets the Berlin strings become the underpainting — dense, even, able to carry every layer of colour.

<!-- fact: The three movements of the Daphnis Suite No. 2 are 'Lever du jour', 'Pantomime', and 'Danse générale'; the opening is unveiled by a flute-and-harp interlude -->

Some say Karajan's Ravel is too "beautiful," too smooth. But "Lever du jour" is meant to be a perfect sunrise — and who would complain that a sunrise is too beautiful?

<!-- fiction: responding to the famous criticism that Karajan's readings are too luxurious -->

## The Other Side of the Sea Is Debussy

The Debussy on the album's second half is a different light. In La Mer, Karajan doesn't describe waves; he describes the *memory* of the sea: every motif a flash of recollection, suspended softly in mid-air by the strings and brass.

<!-- fiction: poetic framing of Karajan's La Mer interpretation -->

<!-- fact: La Mer's three movements are 'De l'aube à midi sur la mer' / 'Jeux de vagues' / 'Dialogue du vent et de la mer' -->

## The BPO's Taiwan Bond Belongs to a Later Era

In Karajan's day the Berliner Philharmoniker never came to Taiwan. Their Taiwan memory was opened by Rattle: November 2005, the first visit, some thirty thousand people packed the plaza outside the National Theater. Twenty years later, in November 2025, Petrenko led the orchestra back to Taipei to celebrate the anniversary.

<!-- fact: The BPO first toured Taiwan in Nov 2005 under Sir Simon Rattle; on Nov 11–13, 2025 Kirill Petrenko led three concerts at Taipei's National Concert Hall for the 20th anniversary -->

Karajan never heard Taipei's applause. But this recording means the sea that once lived in his mind can flood the room of any island, anytime.

<!-- fiction: linking the recording's permanence to Taiwanese listeners -->

## Further Listening

If you love the polished sonority of Karajan and the BPO, Petrenko's 2025 Schoenberg album shows a different temperament of the same pairing in the twenty-first century. And Abbado's 1987 Beethoven Fifth with the same orchestra shows how the BPO, after Karajan, hammered out a different "fate" altogether.
