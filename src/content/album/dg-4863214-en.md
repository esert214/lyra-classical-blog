## A Triumphant Return Two Decades in the Making

Twenty-two years ago, a twenty-one-year-old Hilary Hahn delivered a legendary, laser-accurate Sony account of the Brahms Violin Concerto. Her intonation was miraculous, her focus unyielding.

Two decades later—having navigated marriage, motherhood, artistic sabbaticals, and global stardom—Hahn returned to Brahms in Frankfurt. Under the yellow banner of Deutsche Grammophon, the once-austere prodigy returns as a sovereign master: warmer, deeper, yet retaining that legendary core of steel.

<!-- fact: Released by DG (486 3214), this recording features the Brahms Violin Concerto in D major, Op. 77, alongside the Double Concerto in A minor, Op. 102, with cellist Andreas Brantelid and the hr-Sinfonieorchester -->

## Symphonic Wrestling with Granite and Fire

Historically dubbed a 'concerto against the violin,' Brahms’s masterpiece offers no frivolous virtuoso posturing. It demands a warrior capable of battling a full romantic orchestra while matching its symphonic weight.

Following the massive orchestral opening, Hahn’s entrance strikes like lightning tearing through storm clouds. Her doublestops ring like struck bronze. In the Joachim cadenza, she untangles knotty polyphony with architectural clarity. Yet in the Adagio, when the oboe floats its pastoral solo, her violin weaves around it with radiant tenderness.
