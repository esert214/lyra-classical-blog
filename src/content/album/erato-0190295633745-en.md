## When a Breakdancer Sings with the Voice of Angels

It is impossible to find an artist quite like Jakub Józef Orliński: by afternoon, a street-style b-boy performing headspins in sneakers; by evening, a tuxedo-clad countertenor singing with celestial vocal purity.

This landmark debut recital on Erato, *Anima Sacra*, announced the arrival of a generational superstar. Rather than reciting well-trodden Handel hits, Orliński trawled archives in Naples and the Vatican to unearth eight world-premiere recordings of sacred 18th-century gems by Zelenka, Durante, Feo, and Terradellas.

## Pristine Chords Cascading from Cathedral Domes

In Zelenka’s *Gesù al Calvario* aria *S'una sol lagrima*, Orliński avoids the pinched throatiness that formerly marred the countertenor register. His voice is creamy, luminous, and seamless, like a white pebble polished by mountain streams. Backed by Maxim Emelyanychev and the fiery period strings of Il Pomo d'Oro, his notes descend like shafts of sunlight through stained-glass basilicas.

In Durante’s rapid-fire motets, his coloratura leaps with acrobatic weightlessness, matching the kinetic joy of his breakdancing footwork.

## A Pure Anchorage in a Noisy World

In an age of endless digital clamor, Orliński’s sound possesses an astonishing meditative stillness. He strips away worldly pretension, guiding the listener into an unblemished monastery of the soul.
