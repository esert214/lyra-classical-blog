## A Vinyl Record in Granada

Years ago in a modest living room in Granada, a little girl sat on the carpet listening to an old stereo playing Jascha Heifetz.

<!-- fiction: imagining Dueñas's childhood in Andalusia listening to vintage vinyl -->

The sound sliced through the summer heat like a silver blade—flawless, laser-focused, without an ounce of indulgent sentimentality, yet leaving behind a trail of radiant silver light. That girl was María Dueñas. Years later, when she walked into Abbey Road Studios to record this tribute, she held in her hands the very 1731 Stradivarius "Heifetz, Piel" that Heifetz himself once treasured.

<!-- fact: Portions of this album were recorded at Abbey Road Studios, with Dueñas playing the 1731 Stradivarius violin formerly owned by Jascha Heifetz -->

Certain instruments hold their former master's breath locked inside their varnish. The instant bow met string, it felt as though Heifetz's ghost had struck a match in the air.

<!-- fiction: evocative literary imagery of the historic violin passing between generations -->

## Korngold's Golden Screen and Lalo's Fierce Blood

At the core of the album stand two concertos forever welded to Heifetz's legacy: Erich Wolfgang Korngold's Violin Concerto in D major, and Édouard Lalo's *Symphonie espagnole*.

<!-- fact: Heifetz premiered Korngold's concerto in 1947, cementing its place in the modern canon; Lalo's work was another lifelong Heifetz staple -->

In Korngold's opening movement, Gustavo Dudamel and the Simón Bolívar Symphony unfurl a cinematic sunset from the golden age of Hollywood. When the plush velvet waves of the orchestra swell, Dueñas does not drown. Her upper register possess an uncommon steeliness—not fragile tears, but the resolute stride of a traveler bathed in blinding sunlight.

<!-- fiction: characterizing the balance of orchestral splendor and solo strength -->

In Lalo's *Symphonie espagnole*, the Andalusian blood in her veins catches fire. Her staccato articulations are clean and perilous, like the sweep of a matador's cape scented with gunpowder and the percussive stomps of flamenco.

<!-- fiction: connecting Dueñas's Granada heritage with Lalo's Iberian spirit -->

## Starlight Encores: Saying Goodbye in the Shadows

Alongside the two orchestral pillars, Dueñas joins veteran pianist Itamar Golan for two of Heifetz's signature encore arrangements: Debussy's *Beau Soir* and Ponce's *Estrellita*.

<!-- fact: The album features Heifetz's arrangements of Debussy's Beau Soir and Ponce's Estrellita, performed with pianist Itamar Golan -->

What remains most haunting about Heifetz was never merely the mechanical perfection that earned him the moniker of an impassive technician; it was the quiet melancholy that slipped out during his encores—a solitary titan looking back at an emptied hall as the stage lights died down.

<!-- fiction: humanizing reflection on Heifetz's enigmatic aura -->

Dueñas understands that loneliness. Her *Estrellita* is not an innocent lullaby; it is a letter of gratitude whispered across time to an unreachable star.

<!-- fiction: poetic closing tribute -->
