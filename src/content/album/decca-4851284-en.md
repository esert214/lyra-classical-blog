## A Baroque Wildfire on the Banks of the Nile

Whoever dismissed Baroque opera as courtly powder and dry recitative never heard Il Giardino Armonico under the explosive baton of Giovanni Antonini. In their hands, Handel’s 1724 masterpiece *Giulio Cesare in Egitto* transforms into an acoustic rollercoaster racing at breakneck velocity.

Documenting the sensation of the Salzburg Festival, this three-disc Decca set unites mezzo-soprano superstar Cecilia Bartoli with countertenor icon Philippe Jaroussky. Bartoli's Cleopatra is intoxicating, cunning, and desperately human, while Jaroussky infuses young Sesto with an incandescent, heart-wrenching thirst for vengeance.

<!-- fact: Released by Decca (4851284), this 3-CD set captures Giovanni Antonini leading Il Giardino Armonico with Cecilia Bartoli as Cleopatra and Philippe Jaroussky as Sesto -->

## Coloratura Fireworks and Angelic Laments

Bartoli’s coloratura remains one of the wonders of vocal history. In Cleopatra's tempestuous aria *Da tempeste il legno infranto*, her rapid-fire staccato runs and ornamentation burst forth like glistening gemstones hurled into a gale.

In stunning contrast, Jaroussky’s delivery of *Cara speme* floats with celestial clarity. The otherworldly beauty of his countertenor voice, cradled by the earthy, gut-string bite of Il Giardino Armonico, delivers an emotional blow of pure tragic pathos.

<!-- fact: Premiered in London at the King's Theatre in February 1724, Giulio Cesare is widely considered the peak of Handel's Italian opera career in Britain -->

## Igniting the Past on Our Turntable

Many listeners associate the Baroque with polite museum restraint. This recording incinerates that stereotype.

Antonini’s rhythms are primal, Mediterranean, and dangerously alive. Whenever a newcomer walks into the shop, I drop the needle on this set. They invariably stare in astonishment: three centuries ago, lovers and conspirators loved and bled with the very same incandescent fury.
