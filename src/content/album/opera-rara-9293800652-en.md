## A Forgotten Version

You know Verdi's Simon Boccanegra—but did you know it exists in two versions?

<!-- fiction: opening question -->

Simon Boccanegra premiered in Venice in 1857 to a lukewarm reception. Verdi revised it heavily in 1881, giving us the "revised version" we know today.

<!-- fact: Simon Boccanegra premiered in Venice in 1857 to lukewarm reception; Verdi revised it heavily in 1881 -->

And this Opera Rara recording is the world-premiere recording of the original 1857 version—a leaner, distinct masterpiece, very different from the familiar edition.

<!-- fact: This recording is the world-premiere recording of the 1857 original, released by Opera Rara -->

## The Lean Original

The 1857 original is far leaner than the 1881 revision. It lacks the later-added court scene, is more compact in structure, and retains more of Verdi's "early" style.

<!-- fact: The 1857 original is leaner than the 1881 revision, without the later court scene, retaining more early Verdi style -->

For listeners familiar with the revision, this is a listening experience both familiar and strange—the same plot, but different music.

<!-- fiction: description of the listening experience -->

This version matters not just because it is "rare." It matters because it shows us how Verdi evolved from a conception into the final masterpiece.

<!-- opinion: commentary on philological value -->

## The Famous Council Chamber

The most famous moment in Simon Boccanegra is the "council chamber" scene at the end of Act I—in which Boccanegra, amid a hostile assembly of nobles, reveals his identity.

<!-- fact: The 'council chamber' scene is a famous Act I finale moment, where Boccanegra reveals his identity before the nobles -->

In the 1857 original, this scene is more direct and raw than the revision, without so many later adornments. Alcántara's Boccanegra here shows that inward, profound dignity.

<!-- fiction: description of the original scene and the singing -->
