## Guitar at Dusk

Vesper—dusk. The Latin word also means the hour of evening prayer.

<!-- fact: Vesper means dusk or evening prayer -->

The album's title is well chosen. Because the music inside carries that particular quality of dusk: bright, yet already fading.

<!-- fiction: interpretation of the album's title -->

This is another major release by Scottish guitarist Sean Shibe, featuring works by three British contemporary composers, several of them recorded for the first time.

<!-- fact: Pentatone PTC 5187 518, released 2026, featuring works by three British contemporary composers -->

## Adès's Forgotten Dances

Thomas Adès's Forgotten Dances opens the album and receives its world-premiere recording here.

<!-- fact: Adès's Forgotten Dances is one of the world-premiere recordings on this album -->

Adès's music is renowned for being "modern and nostalgic" at once. In this piece you can hear the skeleton of an antique dance, filled with contemporary harmony and rhythm—like a modern light cast onto an old fresco in a museum.

<!-- fiction: metaphor for the music -->

Shibe's playing brings those "forgotten dances" back to life, at once precise and elastic.

<!-- fiction: description of the performance -->

## Dillon's 12 Caprices

James Dillon's 12 Caprices is also recorded here for the first time.

<!-- fact: Dillon's 12 Caprices is one of the world-premiere recordings on this album -->

Dillon's music is known for its complexity and intricacy. These twelve caprices explore the guitar's full range of colors—from the finest harmonics to the most ferocious strumming.

<!-- fiction: description of the work's character -->

This is not "pretty" guitar music. It is a challenge to the instrument's limits, and a test of the listener's ears.

<!-- opinion: subjective commentary -->

## Birtwistle's Beyond the White Hand

Harrison Birtwistle's Beyond the White Hand appears on the album. Birtwistle, who died in 2022, was a towering figure of British modernism.

<!-- fact: Birtwistle (1934–2022) was a British modernist composer; Beyond the White Hand appears on this album -->

His music is restless and sharp, yet conceals a raw lyricism. In Shibe's reading, that image of "beyond the white hand"—beyond the everyday, beyond the familiar—is vividly rendered.

<!-- fiction: interpretation of the work -->

## Why Listen

This album earned high praise from The Guardian and is regarded as an important milestone in contemporary guitar music.

<!-- fact: The album was praised by The Guardian -->

Shibe is one of the few musicians who commands both classical and electric guitar, and Vesper is a work of his artistic maturity. It is not "background music"—it asks you to sit down and listen closely.

<!-- opinion: subjective recommendation -->

If you're interested in "new music" of the twentieth century and beyond, this is a contemporary guitar masterwork not to be missed.

<!-- opinion: subjective recommendation -->

## Further Listening

For more depth in contemporary music, Denis Kozhukhin's Somnia and Charlotte Hu's Goyescas offer different landscapes for piano.

<!-- fact: Kozhukhin's and Hu's albums are both released on Pentatone -->
