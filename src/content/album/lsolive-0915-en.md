## An Absurd Dream

Janáček rarely wrote comic operas. But The Excursions of Mr Brouček is one.

<!-- fact: The Excursions of Mr Brouček is Janáček's rare comic opera -->

The protagonist, Mr Brouček, is a minor Prague landowner—a vain, boastful, foolish yet endearing philistine. Drunk, he dreams two absurd dreams: flying to the Moon, and being swept back to the 15th-century Hussite Wars.

<!-- fact: The opera tells of Brouček's two drunken dreams: one to the Moon, one to the Hussite era -->

This is a "baffling but beautiful" opera—as The Guardian review put it.

<!-- fact: The Guardian called this opera 'baffling but beautiful' -->

## The Moon's Satire

In the first dream, Brouček arrives on the Moon. Its inhabitants are aesthetes detached from reality, caring only for art and beauty, ignorant of the mundane world.

<!-- fact: In the first dream Brouček visits the Moon, whose inhabitants are aestheticists detached from reality -->

Janáček here uses satire to contrast Brouček's crude pragmatism with the lunar people's ethereal emptiness—two extremes, unable to understand each other.

<!-- fiction: interpretation of the opera's satire -->

## The Seriousness of the Hussites

The second dream is more complex. Brouček is swept into the 15th-century Hussite Wars—a conflict over Czech national faith and freedom.

<!-- fact: In the second dream Brouček is caught up in the Hussite Wars -->

Here the opera's satire turns serious. Brouček, a philistine who only wants a comfortable life, seems tiny and pathetic among people fighting for ideals.

<!-- fiction: interpretation of the opera's theme -->

This lifts the whole work from mere comedy to a profound reflection on "ideals and reality."

<!-- opinion: commentary on the opera's depth -->

## Rattle Embraces the Bizarre

This recording by Simon Rattle and the London Symphony Orchestra was released by LSO Live in 2026.

<!-- fact: LSO Live LSO0915, released 2026 -->

The Guardian described Rattle as "embracing the baffling but beautiful opera's bizarre charms."

<!-- fact: The Guardian said Rattle embraces this opera's bizarre charms -->

Rattle doesn't play this comic opera as a frivolous farce. He preserves the serious undercurrent of Janáček's music, while letting the absurd passages glimmer with humor.

<!-- fiction: commentary on Rattle's interpretation -->

## Why Listen

Among Janáček's operas, Jenůfa and Kát'a Kabanová are better known, but Brouček reveals his lesser-known side—humor, fantasy, and social observation.

<!-- opinion: commentary on Janáček's operatic variety -->

For Janáček fans, this is an unmissable recording. For newcomers, it is an entertaining, unconventional alternative to typical opera.

<!-- opinion: subjective recommendation -->

## Further Listening

If you love Janáček, Rattle's Jenůfa is a more dramatic masterpiece. And his Mahler Seventh offers another musical landscape.
