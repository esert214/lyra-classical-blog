## Dreams on the Piano

"Somnia" is Latin, meaning "dreams."

<!-- fact: Somnia is Latin for 'dreams' -->

And this album is a piano journey about dreams. Russian pianist Denis Kozhukhin, with his sound, interprets the "dreams" depicted by composers including Scriabin, Ravel, Prokofiev, and Tchaikovsky.

<!-- fact: This album is themed on 'dreams,' featuring piano works by Scriabin, Ravel, Prokofiev, Tchaikovsky, and others -->

## Scriabin's Mystery

Scriabin is one of the album's core composers. His music carries a mystical color; those hazy, half-awake harmonies perfectly fit the "dream" theme.

<!-- fact: Scriabin is known for mystical music -->

Kozhukhin's Scriabin has a rich, rounded tone, rendering those complex harmonies both clear and mistily hazy.

<!-- fiction: commentary on Kozhukhin's playing -->

## Ravel's Color

The album also includes Ravel's works. Ravel's piano music is colorful, yet carries a dreamlike distance.

<!-- fact: The album includes Ravel's piano works -->

Kozhukhin makes Ravel's melodies both refined and fluid, like an Impressionist dream.

<!-- fiction: commentary on the Ravel interpretation -->

## Why Listen

Kozhukhin is the 2010 Queen Elisabeth Piano Competition gold medalist, regarded as a leading representative of the Russian piano school today.

<!-- fact: Kozhukhin is the 2010 Queen Elisabeth Competition gold medalist -->

This album was praised by The Classic Review for its "rich, rounded tone." It is an album suited for late-night listening—letting you, in the music, enter a journey of dreams.

<!-- opinion: subjective recommendation -->

## Further Listening

If you love piano music, Charlotte Hu's Goyescas and Leif Ove Andsnes's Liszt Via Crucis are both worth hearing.

<!-- fact: Goyescas is by Charlotte Hu on Pentatone; Liszt's Via Crucis is by Andsnes -->
