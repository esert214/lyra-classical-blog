## A Holy Requiem Under the Reign of Terror

In the entire annals of twentieth-century opera, no finale matches the devastating spiritual impact of Francis Poulenc’s *Dialogues des Carmélites*.

Based on the true martyrdom of sixteen Carmelite nuns of Compiègne during the French Revolution's Reign of Terror, this landmark Erato release under conductor Jérémie Rhorer brings together an all-star French cast: Patricia Petibon, Véronique Gens, Sabine Devieilhe, and Rosalind Plowright. Recorded at the Théâtre des Champs-Élysées, it stands as an unassailable benchmark.

## The Severed Salve Regina

The harrowing final scene remains one of the most chilling concepts in theatrical history. The condemned sisters march calmly toward the guillotine in Revolutionary Paris, singing the sacred hymn *Salve Regina*.

After each verse, a sickening, mechanical crash rings out from the orchestra—the guillotine blade falling. Immediately, one voice drops out of the choral tapestry. With each successive blow, the texture thins until only the timid novice Blanche de la Force remains. Having conquered lifelong dread, she steps forward to sing the final phrases into eternity.
