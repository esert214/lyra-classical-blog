## A Work Devoted to a Lifetime

Bach's Mass in B Minor is not a single work of liturgical music completed at once. It is a summation of Bach's life.

<!-- fiction: opening commentary -->

In his final years, Bach gathered fragments of works he had composed throughout his life—cantatas, organ pieces, and entirely new music—reorganized, revised, and wove them into this monumental Mass.

<!-- fact: The B Minor Mass is a monumental work assembled by Bach in his old age from fragments of his life's output -->

It covers the entire Ordinary of the Mass, lasting over two hours, and is regarded as one of the greatest choral works in Western music.

<!-- fact: The B Minor Mass is Bach's largest choral work, lasting over two hours -->

And this recording by Raphaël Pichon and his Pygmalion period-instrument ensemble was praised by The Classic Review: "hear this version and you know period instruments can be this beautiful."

<!-- fact: This recording was praised by The Classic Review for the beauty of period instruments -->

## The Beauty of Period Instruments

In recent years, "period performance" has often been criticized as "more scholarly than emotional."

<!-- opinion: commentary on the common debate about period performance -->

But Pichon's B Minor Mass completely overturns that impression. Pygmalion's musicians play gut strings and period winds, yet produce a warm, translucent, deeply human sound.

<!-- fiction: commentary on the period-instrument timbre -->

That famous opening Kyrie—the interweaving of low strings and chorus—carries a quality at once solemn and grieving, making you forget the difference between "period" and "modern"—only the music remains.

<!-- fiction: description of the opening music -->

## The Soloists' Radiance

This recording's soloists include soprano Julie Roset, alto Lucile Richardot, tenor Emiliano Gonzalez Toro, and others—all leading figures in today's period vocal world.

Their vocal technique and Pichon's conducting style fit perfectly, making those complex fugues and coloraturas both precise and expressive.

## Why Listen

If you're daunted by Bach's B Minor Mass—because it's too long, too complex, or because the word "period" makes you hesitate—this album will change your mind.

<!-- opinion: subjective recommendation -->

This is a recording that lets you immerse fully and feel the beauty of Bach's music. It is not a display of scholarship, but a presentation of emotion and faith.

<!-- opinion: subjective commentary -->
