## A Supermarket Tabloid Freak

In 1992, the infamous tabloid *Weekly World News* ran a grotesque front-page photo: a half-boy, half-bat creature had been discovered in a subterranean cave in Hope Falls, West Virginia.

<!-- fact: Bat Boy was inspired by a 1992 Weekly World News satirical cover story that became an enduring staple of American tabloid folklore -->

Most readers tossed it into the trash with a smirk. But composer-lyricist Laurence O'Keefe (who would later create *Legally Blonde* and *Heathers*) heard music beneath the ink: what if this creature desperately wanted to be human? What if he possessed a capacity for love deeper than any ordinary citizen in that pious town?

<!-- fact: Laurence O'Keefe, with book writers Keythe Farley and Brian Flemming, adapted the story into a musical that swept Off-Broadway awards in 2001 -->

This August 2026 cast recording from the New York City Center Gala production re-orchestrates the cult masterwork for twelve musicians and features three new songs. Starring Broadway prodigy Taylor Trensch in the titular role, it is nothing short of a miracle.

<!-- fact: Released digitally by Ghostlight Records on August 28, 2026, preserving the City Center gala production with Taylor Trensch and Kerry Butler -->

## From Screeching Bloodsucker to Oxford Gentleman

The first act pivots on the outrageous transformation from feral beast to educated gentleman.

<!-- fact: In the story, veterinarian's wife Meredith Parker takes in Bat Boy and uses BBC instructional audio tapes to teach him impeccable Queen's English -->

The townsfolk want him burned at the stake, but warm-hearted Meredith Parker (the peerless Kerry Butler) shields him. When Edgar puts on a tailored suit and bursts into song in pristine Oxford English, the audience goes from laughing out loud to feeling a sudden ache in their throat.

<!-- fiction: characterizing the tragicomic whiplash of Edgar's metamorphosis -->

He learns how to pour Earl Grey; he learns proper table manners. He assumes that if he is only civil enough, humanity will embrace him. But the cruelty of human prejudice is far sharper than any cave-dweller's fangs.

<!-- fiction: thematic contrast of beastly innocence versus civilized malice -->

## "Comfort and Joy": Shattering the Facade

O'Keefe's score is a dizzying kaleidoscope of 80s glam-rock, soaring gospel chorales, and razor-sharp operatic counterpoint.

<!-- fact: O'Keefe's music deftly navigates pastiche, rock anthems, and dense contrapuntal ensembles -->

During the Act I Christmas finale "Comfort and Joy," town hypocrisy, Dr. Parker's simmering jealousy, Meredith's protective anxiety, and Edgar's earnest prayer collide in a torrential four-way counterpoint.

<!-- fiction: describing the musical momentum and dramatic escalation of the ensemble piece -->

While the climax careens into Jacobean blood tragedy, Edgar's dying plea is devastating.

<!-- fact: The plot borrows heavily from Oedipus Rex and Frankenstein, turning camp satire into classical catharsis -->

There is a Bat Boy inside each of us—longing for connection, terrified that the moment we emerge from our cave, the daylight will burn us to cinder.
