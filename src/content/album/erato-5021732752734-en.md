## A Spiritual Cathedral Built Across Ten Discs

In the minuscule instant when the harpsichord's quill plucks the brass string, three hundred and sixty years of dust dissolve into thin air.

Jean Rondeau's monumental ten-disc recording, *Louis Couperin: The Complete Works*—captured across eight intense months with three historic harpsichords and two ecclesiastical organs—captured the pinnacle of the classical world in autumn 2026, winning both **Gramophone Recording of the Year and the Instrumental Award**. Gramophone rightly hailed it as "one of the most decisive, visionary recording projects of the past decade."

## The Unmeasured Prelude: Free Will Suspended in Air

Louis Couperin died at just thirty-five. Uncle to the illustrious François "le Grand," Louis possessed an aesthetic far wilder, darker, and more metaphysically daring than the polished courtliness of the later Sun King's court.

The summit of this pilgrimage lies in his famed *préludes non mesurés* (unmeasured preludes). On manuscript paper, they appear without bar lines or note stems—a cascade of whole notes bound by delicate curves. It is a constellation chart handed across centuries: where to linger, when to breathe, how long to let the celestial decay linger in silent air is left entirely to the player's immediate soul.

Under Rondeau's hands, the harpsichord breathes like an organic creature. His rubato is neither indulgent nor rigid; it has the unpredictable, hypnotic cadence of rain whispering against autumn windowpanes.

## Deep Within the Pipes of Saint-Gervais

Alongside three distinctive harpsichords, Rondeau journeys to the Église Saint-Gervais in Paris, where Louis Couperin served as titulaire organist until his untimely death in 1661.

When the wooden pedalboard depresses the fundamental pipes, the sound emerges with an ancient, earthy gravitas. Rondeau refuses to domesticate Couperin into powdered porcelain; he returns him to stone chapels, drafty garrets, and solitary nights illuminated only by tallow candles.

<!-- fact: Couperin served as the organist at Saint-Gervais from 1653, establishing a legendary family musical dynasty that spanned nearly two centuries -->

Ten discs demand total surrender. In an era addicted to thirty-second dopamine hits, Rondeau has constructed a timeless sanctuary of sound—an unforgettable monument to human contemplation.
