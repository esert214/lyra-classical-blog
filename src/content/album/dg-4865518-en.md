## When a Russian Soul Meets the Manhattan Skyline

How does a Russian piano prodigy raised on Volga folk songs and severe Moscow conservatory rigors interpret the sprawling, neon-lit horizon of his adopted homeland after a decade in America?

Superstar Daniil Trifonov teams with longtime collaborator Yannick Nézet-Séguin and The Philadelphia Orchestra for *My American Story: North*. Anchored by George Gershwin's vivacious *Concerto in F*, Aaron Copland’s jagged *Piano Variations*, and John Adams's motoric tour-de-force *Century Rolls*, this is an electrifying love letter to the American landscape.

<!-- fact: Released by Deutsche Grammophon in autumn 2024, representing the first chapter in Trifonov’s comprehensive exploration of American musical identity -->

## Lush Philadelphia Velvet and Syncopated Swagger

In Gershwin’s Concerto, Trifonov avoids brittle kitsch, lending the work Rachmaninoff-sized symphonic architecture. While the Charleston syncopations snap with razor sharpness, the nocturnal blues melody of the second movement dissolves into celestial Philadelphia string silk, glowing like the tip of the Empire State Building piercing midnight fog.

In John Adams’s *Century Rolls*—inspired by mechanical player piano rolls—Trifonov’s rhythmic stamina borders on the supernatural. Thousands of rapidly cascading notes swirl with sparkling wit and irrepressible human warmth.

<!-- fact: Gershwin’s Concerto in F premiered in 1925 at Carnegie Hall with the composer at the piano -->

## An Immigrant's Anthem of Freedom

Listening in the quiet of the night, one realizes that the American Dream was always built by wanderers. Trifonov’s dazzling pianism transforms skyscrapers and prairie plains into a universal lullaby for every soul seeking a home.
