## A Fresh Glass of Iced Champagne

If Mozart's *The Marriage of Figaro* is a bittersweet human comedy steeped in forgiveness and melancholy, Gioachino Rossini’s *The Barber of Seville* is a bottle of chilled vintage champagne uncorked with exhilarating abandon on a summer afternoon.

<!-- fact: Composed in just under three weeks in 1816 based on Beaumarchais's play, Rossini's Il barbiere di Siviglia precedes the events of Mozart's Le nozze di Figaro, forming the twin pillars of Italian comic opera -->

The effervescence surges instantly: citrus, bright Mediterranean sunshine, and unapologetic laughter fill the room. In Rossini’s world, no one worries about cosmic despair; every predicament can be dismantled with a barber’s silver tongue, a purse of gold coins, and a machine-gun spray of coloratura pyrotechnics.

<!-- fiction: Evoking the effervescent, infectious comic spirit of Rossini's Seville -->

Recorded in London in 1974 by a thirty-one-year-old James Levine leading the London Symphony Orchestra, this EMI set remains half a century later the benchmark for sheer theatrical exuberance.

<!-- fact: Recorded in August-September 1974 at Abbey Road Studios and released on EMI (CDS 7 47634 8), this legendary recording stars Sills, Milnes, Gedda, Capecchi, and Raimondi under Levine's baton -->

## Bubbles, Coloratura, and Silver High Fs

As Rosina, Beverly Sills—affectionately known across America as "Bubbles"—delivers one of the most radiant portrayals in discographic history.

When she launches into her famous Act I cavatina "Una voce poco fa," the studio air fairly crackles. Sills does not play Rosina as a timid victim of her guardian’s clutches, but as a razor-sharp, sparkling schemer: "I am docile, I am respectful... but if you cross me, I have a hundred traps ready to spring!"

<!-- fact: 'Una voce poco fa' is Rosina's signature entrance aria, where Sills displays her legendary high F ornamentation and effortless bel canto trills -->

Her silver vocal timbre and effortless roulades sparkle like colored marbles bouncing off crystal goblets. Meanwhile, Sherrill Milnes storms through the front door as Figaro with his booming "Largo al factotum," his bronze baritone ringing out with unstoppable swagger as Seville's indispensable fixer.

<!-- fact: Milnes brings heroic vocal weight to Figaro, while Swedish tenor Nicolai Gedda delivers Almaviva's florid divisions with aristocratic grace -->

## The Sovereign Antidote to Gloom

When the London Symphony Orchestra surges beneath Levine’s buoyant baton into those trademark "Rossini crescendos," the accumulated heaviness of modern life simply evaporates.

<!-- fact: The Rossini crescendo is the composer's signature device, building kinetic excitement by repeating a short motif with progressively denser orchestration and rising volume -->

Sometimes we do not seek out music to interrogate our suffering or seek spiritual repentance. Sometimes we simply need two hours in the company of brilliant eccentrics who outwit pompous tyrants, don preposterous disguises, and toast triumphantly in a blaze of high-octane ensemble singing.

<!-- fiction: Wally Records closing reflection on the pure therapeutic joy of opera buffa -->

If your spirits feel grey today, turn on this *Barber of Seville*. From Sills’ very first silvery chuckle, the world will instantly burst back into vivid technicolor.
