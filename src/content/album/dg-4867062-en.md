## The Opera Stalin Condemned

In 1936, the Soviet newspaper Pravda published a notorious article, "Muddle Instead of Music," viciously attacking Shostakovich's opera Lady Macbeth of Mtsensk.

<!-- fact: The 1936 Pravda article 'Muddle Instead of Music' attacked this opera, marking the start of official Soviet repression -->

That article nearly destroyed Shostakovich's life. He was forced to withdraw the opera and fell silent for years.

<!-- fact: The attack led to the opera's withdrawal, and Shostakovich fell silent for years -->

But the opera itself is among the greatest of the 20th century—violent, satirical, and unsettlingly human.

<!-- opinion: commentary on the opera's historical standing -->

## Katerina's Story

Lady Macbeth tells the story of Katerina Izmailova, a merchant's wife. Suffocating in her repressive marriage, she begins an affair with a worker, Sergei, and eventually descends into serial murder.

<!-- fact: The opera tells of Katerina's murderous path born of a repressive marriage -->

Shostakovich shapes this "evil" woman with music that is at once sympathetic and terrifying. She is not a simple demon—she is pushed to the brink by a cannibalistic society.

<!-- fiction: interpretation of the protagonist -->

## Violence and Satire

The opera depicts violence with astonishing music—thundering orchestra, mocking brass, and twisted melodies.

<!-- fiction: description of the musical style -->

But Shostakovich's greatest weapon is its satire. In the darkest moments, he inserts almost comical passages, making the whole story both cruel and absurd. This is his veiled protest against an absurd era.

<!-- fiction: commentary on Shostakovich's style -->

## Nelsons's Command

This recording by Andris Nelsons and the Boston Symphony is a powerful installment in Nelsons's Shostakovich series.

<!-- fact: This is part of Nelsons's Shostakovich series; DG 4867062, released 2025 (recorded live in Boston in January 2024) -->

Nelsons is one of today's finest Shostakovich interpreters. He has unique command over this music—at once violent and satirical, grand and intimate.

<!-- opinion: commentary on Nelsons's interpretation -->

Latvian soprano Kristine Opolais's Katerina has both repressed restraint and explosive passion, perfectly rendering this complex character.

## Why Listen

This is an "important" opera—not only for its musical achievement, but for the heavy history it carries.

<!-- opinion: subjective commentary -->

It was condemned and withdrawn, yet has become a milestone of 20th-century opera. Listening to this recording, you are not just hearing an opera—you are hearing how an artist, under political pressure, kept his humanity and dignity through music.
