## Daybreak and Abyssal Shadows for 120 Musicians

When one hundred and twenty-five musicians hold their breath in unison, the nocturnal curtain descends in suffocating B-flat minor down into the earth's mantle.

Richard Strauss's *An Alpine Symphony* Op. 64 is no casual pastoral stroll; it is the ultimate orchestral Everest—the most brutally demanding summit of orchestration and instrumental endurance in late-Romantic history. In autumn 2026, Vasily Petrenko and the Royal Philharmonic Orchestra unveiled their flagship live recording on Harmonia Mundi, delivering an electrifying testament to symphonic grandeur.

## Cascading Waterfalls and Glacial Peril

From the blazing sunburst of *Sonnenaufgang*, Petrenko demonstrates sovereign mastery over Strauss's colossal textures. Off-stage brass fanfares float through the morning mist like crisp mountain air scented with spruce.

As the trek advances past *Am Wasserfall*, celesta and high woodwinds spray droplets so palpable they seem to mist the listener's brow. Across the treacherous fissures of *Auf dem Gletscher*, icy string overtones evoke blinding, perilous altitude. Petrenko's genius lies in architectural restraint: pacing the climb like a seasoned alpine guide, saving volcanic reserves for the ecstatic summit breakthrough.

## The Roar of the Tempest into Eternal Twilight

On the summit (*Auf dem Gipfel*), the solo oboe sings a trembling hymn of existential awe before the sky bruises black. The wind machine screams, thunder sheets shudder, and the RPO brass hurls thunderbolts through the acoustic hall.

Yet the greatest triumph arrives in the aftermath: as the organ underpins the slow descent through sunset (*Ausklang*), the titanic mountain beast curls back into primeval night. A colossal sonic spectacle that leaves the spirit exalted and breathless.
