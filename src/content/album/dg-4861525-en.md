## Rescued from an Abandoned Summer Cabin

In 2009, a couple renovating a dilapidated, weather-beaten cabin in St. Anne, Illinois, discovered dozens of water-damaged boxes crammed with musical manuscripts. They had stumbled upon the forgotten estate of Florence Beatrice Price (1887–1953).

In 1933, when the Chicago Symphony Orchestra premiered Price's *Symphony in E minor*, history was made: the first symphony by an African-American woman ever performed by a major American orchestra. Yet double barriers of segregation and sexism conspired to bury her genius under decades of neglect.

## The Philadelphia Sound Crowned with Spirituals

Yannick Nézet-Séguin and The Philadelphia Orchestra bestow upon Price the supreme, golden luxury of the Philadelphia Sound. In the dazzling third movement *Juba Dance*, snapping percussion rhythms and bouncy syncopations bring to life the African foot-stomping tradition with irresistible vivacity.

In the solemn Adagio of Symphony No. 3, Price elevates spiritual melodies to celestial symphonic summits. Warm woodwind chorales and organ-like brass voicings shimmer with unyielding dignity and profound spiritual grace.

## A Shining Star Reclaiming Her Sky

Sitting quietly in the shop as the final chords ring out, one is humbled. Justice may tarry for decades, but enduring beauty cannot be erased. Florence Price’s symphonies now claim their rightful, immortal place in the concert hall.
