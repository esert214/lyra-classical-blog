## A Lonely Hero Beneath Emerald Skin

In 2003, at the Gershwin Theatre on Broadway, *Wicked* revolutionized the American musical. Adapting Gregory Maguire’s bestselling novel, composer Stephen Schwartz inverted the beloved lore of *The Wizard of Oz*, turning the hated "Wicked Witch of the West" into Elphaba—an ostracized green-skinned girl who refuses to conform to political deceit.

In Idina Menzel and Kristin Chenoweth, Broadway found its most iconic duo since Ethel Merman and Mary Martin. Their original cast recording became a multi-platinum cultural touchstone for millions worldwide.

## Soaring High in Defying Gravity

The act one finale, *Defying Gravity*, remains the gold standard of theatrical anthems. When Elphaba mounts her broomstick and rejects the Wizard's corrupt regime, singing "And if I'm flying solo, at least I'm flying free," Menzel's brassy belt catapults into the rafters alongside thunderous orchestral swells.

Equally unforgettable is their intimate farewell duet, *For Good*, where Elphaba and Glinda forgive each other: "Because I knew you, I have been changed for good."

<!-- fact: Idina Menzel won the 2004 Tony Award for Best Actress in a Musical for her performance as Elphaba -->

## A Shield for the Misunderstood

The timeless brilliance of *Wicked* lies in its tender empathy for anyone who has ever felt awkward, ugly, or sidelined. As the overture strikes, it bestows the courage to soar beyond society's suffocating gravity.
