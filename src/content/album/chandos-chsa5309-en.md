## An Unbroken Wave of Russian Romantic Splendor

This is the recording that swept classical critical polls upon arrival, capturing the prestigious **BBC Music Magazine Award**.

Re-formed by British maestro John Wilson, the Sinfonia of London is an orchestra comprised entirely of elite section leaders, international soloists, and chamber stars. When musicians of this caliber tackle Sergei Rachmaninoff’s uncut Symphony No. 2 in E minor, the resultant tonal opulence and electrifying precision redefine standard expectations.

## The Clarinet’s Solitary Autumn Soliloquy

Emerging from psychological despair following the disastrous 1897 premiere of his First Symphony, Rachmaninoff poured all his hard-won healing into this second attempt. Its beating heart is the immortal Adagio, introduced by one of the longest, most heart-stopping clarinet solos in Western music.

Under Wilson's aristocratic pacing, the clarinet's golden timbre floats above hushed strings before the entire orchestra joins in wave upon wave of impassioned yearning. Free of vulgar sentimentality, Wilson balances lushness with athletic transparency. In the finale, the celebratory bell tolls and jubilant fanfares showcase Chandos’s state-of-the-art acoustic capture.
