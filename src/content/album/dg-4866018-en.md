## Preludes, the Beginning of Music

The word "prelude" originally meant a short introductory piece before a longer work. But in Chopin's hands, the prelude was elevated into an independent art form.

<!-- fact: The prelude was originally a short introductory piece; Chopin elevated it into an independent form -->

Chopin's 24 Preludes, Op. 28, use the twenty-four major and minor keys to write twenty-four different states of mind—short, yet like a complete world.

<!-- fact: Chopin's 24 Preludes, Op. 28, cover all twenty-four keys; it is core piano repertoire -->

And this album by Jan Lisiecki sets out from Chopin, spanning Bach, Rachmaninoff, Messiaen, and Górecki—a journey through the history of the prelude.

<!-- fact: The album includes preludes by Chopin, Bach, Rachmaninoff, Messiaen, and Górecki -->

## Chopin's Heartbeat

Lisiecki's understanding of Chopin stands out among pianists of his generation.

<!-- opinion: commentary on Lisiecki's Chopin -->

He doesn't play Chopin as "salon music"; he lets each prelude reveal its own emotional world. That famous "Raindrop" prelude he plays with both serenity and deep melancholy.

<!-- fiction: commentary on the performance -->

## One Album, a History

This album's most fascinating aspect is its programming—it doesn't just "throw preludes together," but lets them converse with each other.

<!-- opinion: commentary on the programming -->

From Bach's preludes (Well-Tempered Clavier), to Chopin's Op. 28, to Rachmaninoff's various preludes, to Messiaen's early color-rich preludes, to Górecki's holy-minimalist preludes—you can hear how the prelude genre evolved over three centuries.

<!-- fact: The album spans Bach's Well-Tempered Clavier to Górecki's preludes, showing the genre's evolution -->

## Why Listen
