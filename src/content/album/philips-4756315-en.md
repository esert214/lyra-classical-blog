## A River Flowing Through Five Generations

A British conductor once said that conducting Tchaikovsky's Symphonies 4, 5 and 6 is like walking every road in Russia in one go — the tragic, the fateful, and the lonely triumph. Gergiev turns that sentence into lived experience.

## Three Full-Throttle Live Nights

This Philips 475 6315 gathers three live recordings from different years: No. 4 from the Musikverein, Vienna, October 2002; No. 5 reaching back to the Salzburg Festival in July 1998 (captured by ORF); No. 6 another Musikverein night in September 2004. The pairing of Gergiev and the Vienna Philharmonic — a Russian conductor in dialogue with a German-Austrian orchestra — is the most beautiful tension of the whole set.

<!-- fact: No. 4 recorded Oct 17–21, 2002 at the Musikverein, Vienna; No. 5 recorded Jul 25, 1998 at the Salzburg Festival (ORF recording); No. 6 recorded Sep 2004 at the Musikverein; released by Philips -->

## The Pathétique and Nine Final Days

The Sixth, Pathétique, is Tchaikovsky's last testament: it premiered on October 28, 1893, and nine days later he was gone. Gergiev's Pathétique is not grief — it is the magnificent fearlessness after "fate knocks at the door." The finale does not weep; it is the whole universe saying to you: it is over, but this life was worth it.

## Gergiev and Taiwan: Four Taipei Nights

Gergiev is among the Russian conductors who have visited Taiwan most often: the Mariinsky's first Taipei stop in 2002, Tainan and Taipei in 2014, Wagner's Das Rheingold in October 2016, and a full Swan Lake in July 2019. For Taiwanese fans, his name has for years been synonymous with Mariinsky grandeur.

<!-- fact: Gergiev toured Taiwan four times with the Mariinsky (2002, 2014, Oct 2016 Das Rheingold, Jul 2019 Swan Lake) -->

## Further Listening

If Tchaikovsky's string writing moves you, the Bolshoi Ballet's Swan Lake sets the same musical river dancing in ballet form.
