## A Norwegian Singing the Most Italian Music on Earth

Lise Davidsen is Norwegian. Norway brings to mind fjords, fir trees, Ibsen — nothing at all to do with Verdi's boiling Italian blood.

<!-- fiction: opening with the nationality contrast -->

She made her name on "northern" repertoire: Wagner's Sieglinde, Senta, and Elisabeth, Strauss's Salome and Ariadne. Roles demanding a voice like a glacier — enormous, pure, impassive.

<!-- fact: Davidsen rose to fame in Wagner (Sieglinde, Senta, Tannhäuser's Elisabeth) and Strauss (Salome, Ariadne); born 1987, breakthrough after the 2015 Operalia competition -->

Then in 2023 she sang Elisabeth of Valois in Don Carlo at the Royal Opera House, and the whole house sat stunned: this Norwegian Valkyrie sings Verdi more Italian than the Italians?

<!-- fact: role debut as Elisabeth of Valois in Verdi's Don Carlo at the ROH in 2023 marked her turn toward Italian repertoire -->

This Decca album, released September 11, is her answer to the world: her first recording devoted entirely to Verdi.

<!-- fact: Decca 487 2241, released September 11, 2026, her first all-Verdi recording, with Gardner and the LPO -->

## Five Women, Five Heartbreaks

The album isn't really a "greatest Verdi hits" compilation. Its true subject is five women.

<!-- fiction: identifying the album's real curatorial concept -->

Fifteen tracks from five operas, ordered by Verdi's own chronology: Lady Macbeth of Macbeth — ambition burns her to ash; Amelia of Un ballo in maschera — trembling between love and guilt; Leonora of La forza del destino — a lifetime atoning for a sin she never committed; Elisabeth of Don Carlo — dignity as her only armor; Aida of Aida — torn in half between homeland and lover.

<!-- fact: 15 tracks from Macbeth, Un ballo in maschera, La forza del destino, Don Carlo, and Aida, sequenced chronologically to explore five heroines' emotional worlds -->

The sessions took place in January 2025 at London's Henry Wood Hall, produced by John Fraser — sixty-seven minutes and fifty-two seconds.

<!-- fact: recorded January 2025 at Henry Wood Hall, London, produced by John Fraser, engineered by Philip Siney; total duration 67:52 -->

## Lady Macbeth: The Psychology of Darkness

The five Macbeth excerpts form the album's center of gravity — no surprise, since a week after release Davidsen debuts as Lady Macbeth at the Metropolitan Opera, opening the entire 2026/27 season.

<!-- fact: a week after release (September 22–October 20, 2026), Davidsen debuts as Lady Macbeth at the Met in the season-opening production, with Freddie De Tommaso as Macduff -->

In "La luce langue" she sounds like she's negotiating with her own shadow; in the sleepwalking scene "Una macchia," the voice washes itself whiter and whiter until nothing remains. That's not madness — that's a person watching her own soul leak out drop by drop.

<!-- fiction: literary description of the sleepwalking scene interpretation -->

If you know her Wagner, you'll notice something curious: singing Verdi, Davidsen's voice gets smaller — but the drama gets bigger. Once the Nordic glacier melts, there's a Mediterranean underneath.

<!-- fiction: comparing her Wagner and Verdi interpretations -->

## Gardner: The Man Who Understands Her Best

Edward Gardner and Davidsen go way back — this blog has already covered their Der fliegende Holländer (Decca 4870952): Davidsen as Senta, Gardner conducting. Same team.

<!-- fact: Gardner and Davidsen previously collaborated on Decca's Der fliegende Holländer recording (Davidsen as Senta) -->

Gardner took over the LPO in 2021 and added the Norwegian National Opera directorship in 2024. An Englishman running a Norwegian opera house; a Norwegian singing Italian opera — the two of them picked up the wrong scripts and perform them brilliantly.

<!-- fact: Gardner has been LPO Chief Conductor since 2021 and Norwegian National Opera Music Director since 2024; the LPO has announced Paavo Järvi as Chief Conductor from 2028/29 -->

<!-- fiction: humorous comment on the pair's 'miscast' collaboration -->

The LPO strings sound un-English warm on this disc. In "Morro, ma prima in grazia" — Amelia's farewell to her son — Gardner pulls the orchestra back to nearly nothing, leaving Davidsen alone at the cliff's edge.

## Why Now

Met general manager Peter Gelb calls Davidsen "in a league of her own"; after her Barcelona Isolde debut, The Observer's Fiona Maddocks asked whether she might be "the soprano of the century."

<!-- fact: Met GM Peter Gelb describes Davidsen as 'in a league of her own'; Fiona Maddocks raised the 'soprano of the century' question after her Liceu Isolde debut -->

At the 2027 Salzburg Easter Festival she debuts Brünnhilde in Die Walküre with Petrenko. Wagner not abandoned, Verdi newly conquered — is this Norwegian planning to sing the entire nineteenth century?

<!-- fact: Salzburg Easter Festival 2027 brings her Brünnhilde debut in Die Walküre with Petrenko -->

Apparently so. And we're lucky to be living through her years of conquest.
