## The Last Candle Burning Before Dawn

A nocturne has never been mere lullaby music. In Chopin's hands, the nocturne is a mirror reflecting the soul's most vulnerable tremors when all daylight defenses are stripped away.

Sir Stephen Hough’s landmark Hyperion recording of Chopin’s complete Nocturnes represents the distillation of a lifetime of poetic reflection. Resisting the temptation to drown these works in sentimental rubato, Hough opts for an austere nobility. His tone resembles the crisp, delicate frost of an early London winter morning—cool yet underpinned by quiet warmth.

## Poetic Architecture Beneath Ornamental Silks

Chopin's nocturnal textures abound with cascading fiorituras and florid ornamentation. Under Hough’s immaculate fingers, these decorative flourishes never feel like superficial garnishes; they are the natural catch in the throat, the fleeting hesitation of an inward breath.

In the B-flat minor Nocturne, Op. 9 No. 1, his cantabile melody glides like a silver filament over supple bass lines. When reaching the tumultuous storm of the C-sharp minor Nocturne, Op. 27 No. 1, Hough reveals an iron architectural spine, keeping emotional turbulence anchored in classical proportion.

## Midnight Whispers at Wally Records

When midnight approaches and the streetlamps flicker in the quiet fog outside our shop, this is the record I reach for. The soft click of the stylus finding the vinyl groove sounds like Hough settling onto his piano bench.

This is not easy listening designed for background comfort; it is an intimate confession. Hough’s crystalline playing reminds us that solitude is not an affliction to be fled, but the quiet shore where we finally meet ourselves.
