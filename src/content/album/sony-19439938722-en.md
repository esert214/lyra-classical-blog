## A Fifty-Year Vintage of Inseparable Camaraderie

In the history of classical chamber music, partnerships spanning over half a century are rarer than rubies.

Cellist Yo-Yo Ma and pianist Emanuel Ax first made music together in 1973 at the Marlboro Music Festival. Their Sony Classical release, subtitled *Hope Amid Tears: Complete Works for Cello and Piano*, marks their fourth comprehensive traversal of Beethoven’s five cello sonatas and three variation sets.

<!-- fact: Released by Sony Classical (19439938722) across three discs, this milestone album captures Yo-Yo Ma and Emanuel Ax performing all of Beethoven’s works for cello and piano -->

## Liberating the Cello into Sovereign Dialogue

Beethoven virtually invented the modern cello sonata. Prior to his Op. 5, the cello remained trapped in continuo servitude; Beethoven elevated it into an equal conversational partner with the keyboard.

In the sunny, noble opening of Sonata No. 3 in A major, Op. 69, Ma’s 1712 'Davidov' Stradivarius sings with effortless wisdom. Ax’s entrance follows like the finishing of a shared sentence. By the time they navigate the knotty contrapuntal fugue of the final Sonata in D major, Op. 102 No. 2, their playing transcends technical mechanics, glowing with profound spiritual fellowship.

<!-- fact: Yo-Yo Ma plays the storied 1712 'Davidov' Stradivarius cello, formerly played by Russian legend Karl Davidov and British virtuoso Jacqueline du Pré -->

## Comfort by the Fireside at Wally Records

There are no aggressive showpiece antics here, no self-conscious efforts to shock. This is the intimate fireside conversation of two lifelong companions who have mastered life and art together.

In an anxious world, dropping the needle on this set brings soothing reassurance. Through every measure, Ma and Ax remind us that while grief may be inevitable, music keeps the flame of hope alive.
